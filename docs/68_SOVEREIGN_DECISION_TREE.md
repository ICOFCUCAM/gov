# CivicOS — Sovereign Adoption Decision Tree (Companion 68)

This companion is a structured decision aid for sovereigns considering CivicOS adoption — or any major DPI deployment. It complements Companion 03 (country playbook), Companion 60 (comparative analysis), Companion 62 (city-scale fast track), and Companion 19 (open standards) by providing a sequenced set of questions to walk through before, during, and after adoption.

The thesis: **most failed digital government deployments failed at decisions made before any code was written**. Strategic clarity, political alignment, civil society engagement, and institutional commitments are more determinative than technical architecture. This decision tree helps sovereigns think through the choices honestly before committing.

The discipline: structured questions, honest answers, deliberate decisions; no decision tree substitute for political work; the tree helps prevent common failures, not guarantee success.

---

## 1. The decision sequence

The tree has four main stages:

1. **Should we deploy CivicOS at all?**
2. **If yes, what scope?**
3. **If we proceed, how do we structure governance?**
4. **As we operate, how do we sustain it?**

Each stage has multiple decision points. Honest answers shape the path.

---

## 2. Stage 1: Should we deploy CivicOS at all?

### 2.1 Question: Do we have political will?

Sub-questions:
- Will head of state or PM sponsor for at least 5 years?
- Will the program survive likely political transitions?
- Will civil society be engaged genuinely?
- Will constitutional officers be established or strengthened?

**If yes to most**: proceed to next decision.
**If no to most**: not yet. Build political conditions first; consider city-scale (Companion 62) as wedge while building.
**If mixed**: foundational engagement only — identity, payments, basic data exchange. Defer broader scope.

### 2.2 Question: Do we have constitutional readiness?

Sub-questions:
- Is there a digital-government law or path to one?
- Is the data protection law in force or imminent?
- Is the AI authority established or willing?
- Are independent constitutional officers possible (algorithmic ombudsman, etc.)?

**If yes to most**: proceed.
**If no to most**: build legal architecture first; foundational engagement may proceed in parallel.

### 2.3 Question: Do we have capacity?

Sub-questions:
- Is there sufficient ICT talent in country (with paths to grow)?
- Can we sustain financially (multi-year)?
- Are there local integrators who can be certified?
- Is sovereign cloud or acceptable hosting available?

**If yes to most**: proceed.
**If no to most**: foundational engagement with strong capacity-building component; defer broader scope.

### 2.4 Question: Do we have civil society and oversight readiness?

Sub-questions:
- Is civil society active and protected?
- Is press freedom intact?
- Is judicial independence intact?
- Are auditor general and similar oversight institutions independent?

**If yes to most**: proceed.
**If no to most**: capacity at risk; if proceeding, focus on most narrowly defined modules with maximum constitutional protection.

### 2.5 Decision: Adopt or defer

Based on the four readiness assessments:

- **All four green**: full engagement appropriate.
- **Three green, one amber**: full engagement with strong attention to the amber area.
- **Two green, two amber/red**: foundational engagement only; broader scope deferred until conditions improve.
- **Mostly red**: not yet; revisit when conditions change.

## 3. Stage 2: If yes, what scope?

### 3.1 Question: National or sub-national?

Sub-questions:
- Is national leadership aligned?
- Are sub-national units (cities, states) demanding?
- Is constitutional structure federal or unitary?

**National-led**: standard country playbook (Companion 03).
**City-led**: city-scale fast track (Companion 62).
**Mixed/federated**: sub-national plus national in parallel, with federation planned.

### 3.2 Question: Which modules first?

Standard wedge sequence:
1. Identity (foundational; required for almost everything else).
2. Payments (often visible; revenue-generating downstream).
3. Civil registration (births, deaths — universal experience).
4. Tax (revenue payback; political win).
5. Welfare (visible benefit to citizens; multilateral co-funding likely).

After these, sovereigns choose based on local priorities.

### 3.3 Question: Africa-first profile required?

Sub-questions:
- Is mobile penetration high but smartphone penetration mixed?
- Is connectivity intermittent in rural areas?
- Is power reliability variable?
- Is multilingual coverage essential?

**Yes to most**: Africa-first deployment profile (Companion 08) applies; offline-first; USSD twin; agent network; etc.
**Partial**: hybrid — Africa-first profile in vulnerable regions, urban-optimized in cities.

### 3.4 Question: Indigenous and self-determined community engagement?

Sub-questions:
- Are there indigenous or self-determined communities in the sovereign?
- Are there constitutional or statutory frameworks for their sovereignty?
- Are there past harms requiring reparative posture?

**Yes**: Companion 36 applies; FPIC required; community-led pace and process; consider community-specific deployment options.

### 3.5 Question: Conflict-affected context?

Sub-questions:
- Is there active or recent conflict?
- Are there post-conflict reconciliation processes?
- Are there fragile peace conditions?
- Are there indigenous or minority groups previously targeted?

**Yes**: Companion 49 applies; do-no-harm primary; anti-targeting safeguards; civil-liberties protections enhanced.

### 3.6 Decision: Define scope

Based on assessments, define:
- Geographic scope (national/sub-national/city pilot).
- Module sequence (first 5 modules; subsequent prioritized).
- Profile (Africa-first/standard/hybrid).
- Community engagement protocols (FPIC where applicable).
- Conflict-context safeguards (where applicable).

## 4. Stage 3: If we proceed, how do we structure governance?

### 4.1 Question: Constitutional officer structure?

Sub-questions:
- Will Algorithmic Ombudsman be established?
- Will Sovereign Trust Officer be established?
- Will People's Editor or equivalent be established?
- Will Future Generations Commissioner be established?

**All yes**: full constitutional officer architecture.
**Partial**: prioritize Algorithmic Ombudsman first; phase others.
**Concerns about feasibility**: minimum is independent oversight (likely Auditor General's Algorithmic Office) plus Algorithmic Ombudsman.

### 4.2 Question: Civil society engagement structure?

Sub-questions:
- Will Citizens' Council be seated?
- Will standing civil society access be granted?
- Will Citizens' Assemblies be convened for major decisions?

**All yes**: full civil society engagement.
**Partial**: minimum is Citizens' Council with structural standing access.

### 4.3 Question: Vendor and integrator structure?

Sub-questions:
- Will multiple vendors be procured for critical components?
- Will source escrow be established with sovereign custodians?
- Will local integrators be certified?
- Will civic-tech ecosystem be supported?

**All yes**: anti-lock-in structure intact.
**Partial**: prioritize sovereign keys with state, source escrow, multi-vendor for critical components.

### 4.4 Question: Constitutional anchoring?

Sub-questions:
- Will inclusion floor be constitutionalized?
- Will sovereign keys with state be anchored?
- Will the seven invariants be reflected in law?
- Will the forbidden list be statutorily recognized?

**All yes**: strong constitutional anchoring.
**Partial**: prioritize inclusion floor and sovereign keys at minimum.

### 4.5 Decision: Establish governance architecture

Define:
- Constitutional officers and statutory framework.
- Civil society engagement bodies.
- Vendor structure (multi-vendor, escrow, local integrators).
- Constitutional anchoring of platform commitments.

## 5. Stage 4: As we operate, how do we sustain it?

### 5.1 Question: Talent strategy?

Sub-questions:
- Is Civic Academy operating in country?
- Are universities producing platform engineers?
- Are diaspora reintegration programs active?
- Are public service compensation and respect competitive?

**All yes**: sovereign capability transfer on track.
**Partial**: prioritize Civic Academy; build in-country engineering capacity.

### 5.2 Question: Sustainability practices?

Sub-questions:
- Is multi-year financial commitment sustained?
- Are operating costs covered by revenue modules within target time?
- Is technical debt tracked and remediated?
- Are sunset and exit drills annual?

**All yes**: sustainability discipline operating.
**Partial**: prioritize annual exit drill and capability sunset processes.

### 5.3 Question: Continuous evaluation?

Sub-questions:
- Is continuous AI evaluation operating?
- Are constitutional officers issuing annual reports?
- Are Citizens' Assemblies convened on substantive matters?
- Is era review planned (decadally)?
- Is the standing question asked publicly each year?

**All yes**: living governance.
**Partial**: prioritize standing question and constitutional officer reporting.

### 5.4 Question: Cross-sovereign cooperation?

Sub-questions:
- Are cross-sovereign protocols (Companion 15) being adopted where beneficial?
- Are sovereign exit options preserved in protocols?
- Are bilateral and multilateral arrangements proceeding with sovereignty discipline?

**All yes**: international engagement on sovereign terms.
**Partial**: prioritize identity and payments cross-border where economically valuable; defer more contested protocols.

### 5.5 Decision: Sustain operations

Define:
- Talent strategy with KPIs.
- Sustainability practices including exit drills.
- Continuous evaluation cadence.
- Cross-sovereign engagement strategy.

## 6. Common decision points: red flags

These conditions should pause adoption or scope expansion:

### 6.1 Erosion of constitutional officers

- If constitutional officers are weakened, captured, or politicized, escalate immediately.
- Pause expansion until restored.
- Engage Foundation and civil society.

### 6.2 Surveillance creep

- If forbidden capabilities are proposed (mass surveillance, citizen scoring, etc.), refuse.
- Activate Sovereign Trust Officer if necessary.
- Engage civil society and international observers.

### 6.3 Vendor capture

- If single vendor becomes irreplaceable for critical components, address immediately.
- Activate multi-vendor procurement.
- Test exit options.

### 6.4 Exclusion floor breach

- If inclusion KPI declines significantly, pause scope expansion.
- Investigate root cause.
- Remediate.

### 6.5 Civil society suppression

- If civil society engagement is undermined or suppressed, escalate.
- Engage Foundation and international observers.
- Consider sovereign exit options if pattern persists.

### 6.6 Foreign coercion

- If foreign governments pressure for data sharing or capability constraints, refuse without local court order.
- Activate Sovereign Trust Officer.
- Engage diplomatic and international channels.

### 6.7 Election integrity threat

- If election infrastructure or integrity is at risk, escalate immediately.
- Activate constitutional officers.
- Engage civil society and international observers.

## 7. Common decision points: green flags

These conditions support scope expansion:

### 7.1 Constitutional officers operating effectively

- Algorithmic Ombudsman investigations leading to remediation.
- Sovereign Trust Officer reports respected.
- Civil society engagement substantive.
- Future Generations Commissioner influencing decisions.

### 7.2 Citizens' Assemblies productive

- Outputs implemented within statutory windows.
- Cross-political cooperation.
- Substantive engagement with policy.

### 7.3 Sustainability strong

- Operating costs covered.
- Talent pipeline filling.
- Annual exit drills succeeding.
- Sunset processes functioning.

### 7.4 Cross-sovereign cooperation working

- Treaty arrangements producing value.
- Sovereign opt-outs respected.
- Equitable participation.

### 7.5 Political transitions handled

- Successive administrations honor constitutional anchoring.
- Inclusion floor preserved across transitions.
- Forbidden list expanded, not eroded.

## 8. The standing question for adoption

Each year, leadership should ask publicly:

> "Is what we have built still serving the people, the constitution, and the future? What do we need to change, pause, or undo? Should we expand, maintain current scope, or contract?"

The answer informs scope decisions for the next year. Honest answers matter more than optimistic ones.

---

## 9. The decision tree north star

Adoption decisions are political, institutional, and ethical before they are technical. The decision tree helps sovereigns think through choices honestly. It does not substitute for political work, civil society engagement, or constitutional commitment.

When sovereigns proceed with adoption with clear-eyed assessment of readiness, scope, governance, and sustainability — they earn the prospect of successful deployment.

When sovereigns proceed without honest assessment, ignoring red flags, weakening constitutional commitments for political expedience, or treating CivicOS as quick fix — they will produce damage, not service.

The discipline is honest assessment. The structure is sequential. The judgment is contextual. The decisions are deliberate.

This tree is for sovereigns. The adoption decisions ultimately belong to citizens through their political institutions. The platform serves their decisions; it does not substitute for them.

When in doubt, consult Companion 26 (failure case studies), Companion 58 (limitations and humility), and Companion 43 (synthesis). The tree is one tool; honest reflection across the corpus is the broader work.
