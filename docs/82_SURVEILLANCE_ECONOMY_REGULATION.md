# CivicOS — Surveillance Economy Regulation (Companion 82)

This companion specifies how CivicOS approaches the regulation of the broader surveillance economy — data brokers, behavioral advertising, surveillance-based business models, location data markets, and the commercial extraction of personal data. It complements Companion 24 (security/rights), Companion 22 §8 (privacy), Companion 32 (BAI), Companion 34 (markets), Companion 17 (wallet privacy), and the per-RP UID and sealed compartment patterns throughout the corpus by going deep on the regulatory and structural design of platform engagement with commercial surveillance.

The thesis: **the surveillance economy is the largest threat to civic privacy in the digital age, and a state platform that does not regulate it has left citizens exposed**. CivicOS supports structural commitments against commercial extraction of citizen data, anti-data-broker discipline, behavioral advertising restrictions, location data protection, default-private settings, and citizen control over commercial data use. The platform's per-RP UID design prevents commercial cross-service correlation; this companion extends the discipline to the broader commercial environment.

The discipline: commercial extraction of citizen data tightly limited; data brokers regulated or banned; behavioral advertising restricted in civic contexts; location data protected; default settings privacy-preserving; citizen control over commercial data; anti-discrimination in commercial profiling; children's commercial protection absolute; cross-border data flows limited.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Citizens own their data.** Commercial use requires explicit, informed consent.
2. **Anti-data-broker discipline.** Data brokers regulated or banned per sovereign choice.
3. **Behavioral advertising restricted.** Especially in civic contexts.
4. **Location data protected.** Strong privacy regime.
5. **Default-private settings.** Citizens opt in, not opt out.
6. **Anti-discrimination in commercial profiling.** Prohibited categories enforced.
7. **Children's commercial protection absolute.** No exceptions.
8. **Cross-border commercial data flows limited.** Sovereign authority preserved.
9. **Surveillance-based business models challenged.** Where they undermine rights.
10. **Civil society and citizens have standing.** To challenge commercial surveillance.

---

## 2. Data brokers

### 2.1 The problem

Data brokers aggregate personal information from many sources and sell it. They operate largely without citizen knowledge or meaningful consent. Their data fuels behavioral advertising, profiling, and discrimination.

### 2.2 Mechanisms

- Data broker registration where they exist.
- Disclosure requirements (what data, what sources, what buyers).
- Citizen access to data brokers hold about them.
- Citizen right to correct or delete.
- Restriction or ban on certain data categories (health, location, financial).
- Anti-discrimination enforcement.

### 2.3 Discipline

- Sovereign authority to ban or restrict.
- Anti-circumvention through subsidiaries or foreign operators.
- Cross-sovereign cooperation under treaty.

### 2.4 Forbidden

- Sale of children's data through brokers.
- Sale of health, financial, location data without explicit consent and narrow purpose.
- Sale of data for discriminatory profiling.
- Use of data brokers to circumvent privacy regulations.

---

## 3. Behavioral advertising

### 3.1 The problem

Behavioral advertising tracks users across sites and contexts to target ads. The infrastructure (cookies, fingerprinting, identifiers) enables broader surveillance.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Restriction or ban on cross-site tracking.
- Default-private settings.
- Consent requirements (meaningful, not dark-patterned).
- Anti-fingerprinting protections.
- Limits on targeting categories.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Per-RP UID extends to commercial contexts where possible.
- Anti-correlation across services.
- Children's protection absolute.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Behavioral advertising on civic surfaces (per Companion 12 §11).
- Targeting based on protected characteristics.
- Targeting children.
- Cross-border tracking circumventing sovereign restrictions.

---

## 4. Location data

### 4.1 The principle

Location data is among the most sensitive personal data. Strong protection.

### 4.2 Mechanisms

- Default-off location sharing in apps.
- Granular consent for location use.
- Restrictions on commercial location data sale.
- Anonymization requirements.
- Anti-aggregation from multiple sources.

### 4.3 Discipline

- Anti-tracking-by-default.
- Anti-pattern-recognition that re-identifies.
- Children's location protected absolutely.

### 4.4 Forbidden

- Sale of identifiable location data.
- Use of location data for surveillance of lawful activity (protests, religious gatherings, etc.).
- Cross-border location data flows without consent.
- Children's location data commercial use.

---

## 5. Default-private settings

### 5.1 The principle

Privacy as default. Citizens opt in, not opt out.

### 5.2 Mechanisms

- Civic Wallet defaults privacy-preserving.
- Per-RP UID by default.
- Cookie/tracking opt-out by default in browsers.
- App permissions default-restrictive.
- Marketing opt-in, not opt-out.

### 5.3 Discipline

- Anti-dark-patterns in consent.
- Symmetric opt-in/opt-out (per Companion 12 §11).
- Plain language in consent requests.
- Granular control.

### 5.4 Forbidden

- Dark patterns in consent.
- Pre-checked opt-ins.
- Friction-engineered opt-outs.
- Bundled consents that cannot be granular.

---

## 6. Anti-discrimination in commercial profiling

### 6.1 The principle

Commercial profiling that produces discriminatory outcomes is prohibited.

### 6.2 Mechanisms

- Algorithmic credit, insurance, employment decisions audited (per Companion 10).
- Anti-discrimination law applied to algorithmic decisions.
- Citizens' right to know they were profiled.
- Citizens' right to challenge profiling.
- Civil society standing to challenge patterns.

### 6.3 Discipline

- Stratified outcome reporting.
- Algorithmic Ombudsman scrutiny.
- Cross-service profiling prevented.

### 6.4 Forbidden

- Algorithmic profiling discriminating based on protected characteristics.
- Profiling for adverse decisions without disclosure.
- Profiling that affects fundamental rights without due process.
- Profiling of children for adverse decisions.

---

## 7. Children's commercial protection

Per Companion 22 §13, Companion 37 §3, Companion 39 §11:

### 7.1 The principle

Absolute prohibition on commercial use of children's data.

### 7.2 Mechanisms

- Children's data sealed compartments.
- No commercial advertising directed at children in civic contexts.
- Strict regulation of commercial advertising to children generally.
- Anti-data-collection by children-targeted services.
- Anti-profiling of children for commercial purposes.

### 7.3 Discipline

- Algorithmic Ombudsman enforcement.
- Civil society standing.
- International cooperation on children's commercial protection.

### 7.4 Forbidden absolutely

- Commercial use of children's data, ever.
- Behavioral advertising to children.
- Children's profiles sold to data brokers.
- Children's data crossing borders for commercial use.
- EdTech monetization of children (Companion 39 §12).

## 8. Cross-border commercial data flows

### 8.1 The principle

Citizen commercial data flowing across borders raises sovereignty concerns.

### 8.2 Mechanisms

- Sovereign authority over commercial data residency.
- Cross-border data flow regulations.
- Adequacy assessments for receiving jurisdictions.
- Citizen consent for international data transfers.

### 8.3 Discipline

- Sovereign opt-outs preserved.
- Anti-circumvention through data localization.
- Anti-coercion in international data flow agreements.

### 8.4 Forbidden

- Cross-border data flows exposing citizens to surveillance regimes.
- Cross-border children's data flows commercial.
- Cross-border data flows that violate sovereign consent regimes.
- Trade agreements compromising sovereign data sovereignty.

## 9. Surveillance-based business models

### 9.1 The principle

Business models built on extracting and selling personal data are challenged where they undermine rights.

### 9.2 Approaches

- Sovereign discretion: ban, regulate, or accept.
- Civil society and citizen advocacy.
- Anti-monopoly action against surveillance giants.
- Public alternatives to surveillance services.
- Fiduciary duty frameworks for personal data holders.

### 9.3 Discipline

- Anti-monopoly enforcement.
- Anti-acquisition that consolidates surveillance.
- Anti-coercion through digital service dependency.

### 9.4 Forbidden

- State support for surveillance-based business models that violate citizen rights.
- Use of CivicOS infrastructure to enable surveillance economy.
- Acquisition of surveillance services for state purposes.

---

## 10. Civil society and citizen standing

### 10.1 The principle

Civil society and citizens can challenge commercial surveillance.

### 10.2 Mechanisms

- Class actions on data violations.
- Civil society litigation.
- Algorithmic Ombudsman investigations of commercial surveillance affecting citizens.
- Citizens' Assembly review of major commercial surveillance issues.
- DPA enforcement on commercial actors.

### 10.3 Discipline

- Strong remedies (compensation, injunctions, structural change).
- Class action mechanisms accessible.
- Anti-arbitration-as-cover.

### 10.4 Forbidden

- Restrictions on civil society challenging commercial surveillance.
- Mandatory arbitration foreclosing citizen remedies.
- Suppression of journalism on commercial surveillance.

- No commercial advertising on civic surfaces.

### 11.2 Civic surfaces

- Per-RP UID.
- Sealed compartments.

- Civic-tech and public-good apps prioritized.

### 11.4 Forbidden

- CivicOS partnerships with data brokers.
- CivicOS marketplace apps engaging in surveillance economy practices on citizen data.
- Use of platform data for commercial purposes.

## 14. The surveillance economy regulation north star

The surveillance economy is the largest threat to civic privacy in the digital age. CivicOS supports structural regulation: data broker discipline, behavioral advertising restriction, location data protection, default-private settings, anti-discrimination in commercial profiling, children's commercial protection absolute, sovereign cross-border data authority, and challenges to surveillance-based business models that violate rights.

When CivicOS becomes complicit in surveillance economy practices — through partnerships with data brokers, hosting of surveillance services, weak consent regimes, or commercial use of citizen data — it has failed citizen privacy. Capability without surveillance economy regulation is not progress; it is the institutionalization of commercial surveillance dressed as innovation.

When the platform supports strong surveillance economy regulation — through structural protections, anti-discrimination, children's absolute protection, sovereign cross-border authority, civil society standing — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies that protect citizens from commercial surveillance.

The discipline is daily. The protection is structural. The children's protection is absolute. The civil society standing is real. The sovereign authority is preserved.

The surveillance economy will not regulate itself. The platform's role is to support sovereign regulation, civic discipline, and citizen empowerment. Anything less abandons citizens to commercial surveillance that undermines the very privacy the platform was meant to protect.
