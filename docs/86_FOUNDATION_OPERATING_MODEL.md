# CivicOS — Foundation Operating Model (Companion 86)

This companion specifies the operating model of the CivicOS Foundation in practical operational detail. It complements Companion 19 §7 (Foundation overview), Companion 20 (sustainability/exit), Companion 41 (memory), and Companion 61 (how to fork) by going deep on how the Foundation actually works day-to-day, governs itself, manages decisions, sustains itself financially, and remains accountable.

The thesis: **the Foundation is the structural guarantee of openness and sovereignty preservation that the platform depends on**. Its credibility — and therefore the platform's credibility — depends on its actual operating model: who decides, how, with what oversight, with what protection from capture. This companion is for Foundation board members, operational staff, sovereign customer representatives, civil society members on Foundation bodies, and anyone considering joining or working with the Foundation.

The discipline: structural independence from any single steward, sovereign, or commercial party; multi-stakeholder governance; transparent decision-making; sustainable operations; rotating leadership; strong accountability mechanisms; alignment with platform commitments throughout.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Structural independence.** From CivicOS Inc., from any single sovereign, from commercial parties.
2. **Multi-stakeholder governance.** Sovereigns, civil society, technical community, integrators all represented.
3. **Transparent decision-making.** Decisions documented; processes public.
4. **Sustainable operations.** Financial model that doesn't compromise mission.
5. **Rotating leadership.** Term limits; anti-personality-cult.
6. **Strong accountability.** To members, to civil society, to sovereign customers' citizens.
7. **Aligned with platform commitments.** Foundation embodies what the platform commits to.
8. **Anti-capture across dimensions.** No capture by sovereigns, vendors, or commercial parties.
9. **International scope, sovereign respect.** Operates across sovereigns; respects each.
10. **Conservative growth.** Capacity sustainable; mission-focused.

## 2. Foundation structure

### 2.1 Members

Multi-stakeholder membership:

- **Sovereign customer governments** (voting): primary customers of the platform.
- **CivicOS Inc.** (voting, but limited): commercial steward.
- **Sovereign integrators** (non-voting observer status if not member sovereigns): regional integrator firms.
- **Civil society organizations** (non-voting observer): accredited civil society.
- **Academic institutions** (non-voting observer): universities and research bodies.
- **Multilateral organizations** (non-voting observer): World Bank, regional development banks, etc.

Voting weighted to ensure sovereign customer dominance balanced by anti-single-sovereign capture.

### 2.2 Board

- Elected by members.
- Multi-sovereign representation required.
- Civil society and academic seats reserved.
- Term limits (typically 4 years; renewable once).
- Rotating chair.
- Diverse geographic, cultural, demographic representation.

### 2.3 Operational leadership

- Executive Director: appointed by board; non-renewable single term to prevent personality cult.
- Senior staff: hired through transparent processes.
- Standards body coordinator.
- Civic Academy coordinator.
- Marketplace coordinator.
- Ethics and accountability office.
- Communications.
- Finance.
- Legal.

### 2.4 Standing committees

- **Standards Body** (per Companion 19 §3.1): standards approval and management.
- **Ethics Committee**: alignment with platform commitments.
- **Finance Committee**: budget oversight.
- **Membership Committee**: admissions and standing.
- **Foundation-Inc. Coordination Committee**: managing relationship with commercial steward.
- **Sovereign Customer Council**: ongoing dialogue with sovereign customers.
- **Civil Society Advisory Council**: ongoing dialogue with civil society.

## 3. Decision-making

### 3.1 Routine decisions

- Operational matters: by Executive Director within Board-approved policies.
- Standards approvals: by Standards Body per RFC process (Companion 19 §3.2).
- Membership decisions: by Membership Committee with Board ratification.

### 3.2 Significant decisions

- Major policy changes: by Board with member consultation.
- Financial decisions above threshold: by Board.
- Strategic direction: by Board with multi-stakeholder consultation.

### 3.3 Constitutional decisions

- Foundation charter changes: supermajority Board vote + member ratification.
- Dissolution: qualified member majority with successor designation.
- Trademark changes: supermajority Board vote.

### 3.4 Discipline

- Decisions documented (ADR-style for substantial matters).
- Civil society and sovereign customer input genuinely considered.
- Anti-rubber-stamp culture.
- Recorded votes.

### 3.5 Forbidden in decision-making

- Single-sovereign or single-commercial capture.
- Hidden decision-making.
- Bypassing of standing committees.
- Personality-driven governance.

- Multilateral grants (where mission-aligned).
- Project-specific funding (where mission-aligned).
- Service revenue (training, certification).

### 4.2 Forbidden revenue

- Funding requiring policy alignment compromising mission.
- Commercial advertising.
- Surveillance economy partnerships.
- Foreign government funding requiring sovereign customer adverse decisions.

### 4.3 Discipline

- Diverse funding sources to prevent capture.
- Transparent funding disclosures.
- Independent audit.
- Anti-mission-creep through funding.
- Multi-year financial planning.

### 4.4 Reserves

- Reserves to weather funding fluctuations.
- Reserves for sovereign exit support to customers in transition.
- Reserves for legal defense if needed.

## 10. Foundation-Inc. relationship

### 10.1 The principle

Foundation governs; Inc. operates commercially. Structural separation enforced by founding documents.

### 10.2 Mechanisms

- Inc. participates as one member among many.
- Inc. cannot capture Foundation board.
- Inc. cannot dictate standards.
- Inc. subject to same conformance and certification processes as other implementations.
- Foundation can publicly disagree with Inc. when warranted.

### 10.3 Discipline

- Anti-conflict-of-interest between Foundation and Inc.
- Transparent financial relationship.
- Independent dispute resolution.

### 10.4 Forbidden

- Inc. capture of Foundation.
- Foundation deference to Inc. on standards or governance.
- Hidden financial dependencies.

## 18. The Foundation operating model north star

The CivicOS Foundation is the structural guarantee that the platform's openness and sovereignty preservation are real, not just marketing. Its credibility depends on its actual operating model: multi-stakeholder governance, transparent decision-making, sustainable operations, rotating leadership, strong accountability mechanisms.

When the Foundation becomes captured by single sovereign, single vendor, single commercial party, single personality — it has failed the platform's openness commitment. The platform's other safeguards depend on Foundation independence. Foundation capture is platform capture.

When the Foundation operates with discipline — multi-stakeholder governance, transparent decisions, sustainable finances, rotating leadership, civil society engagement, standards body integrity — it earns the right to be the structural guarantor of an open civic infrastructure ecosystem.

The discipline is daily. The independence is structural. The transparency is honest. The accountability is real. The succession is regular.

Foundations that work like this are rare in the world; most institutions drift toward capture, calcification, or personality-driven governance. CivicOS Foundation is built to resist these drifts and to reform when it doesn't.

The platform's commitments live in many places. They live especially in this Foundation, because if the Foundation fails, the open kernel pledge weakens, the standards governance weakens, the fork pledge weakens, the platform's claim to be openable, replaceable, and trustworthy weakens.

Foundation work is unglamorous, multi-decade, and structurally important. The people who do it well preserve something larger than themselves.
