# CivicOS — Public Broadcasting Deep Dive (Companion 118)

This companion specifies public broadcasting infrastructure and operations. It complements Companion 53 §3 (overview), Companion 72 §6 (press freedom dimensions), Companion 35 (knowledge commons), Companion 39 (lifelong learning), Companion 25 (multilingual), and Companion 51 (religious affairs) by being specifically about the operational design of public broadcasting where it exists.

The thesis: **public broadcasting is civic infrastructure when it functions independently — providing cultural, educational, civic, and informational content beyond commercial logic**. CivicOS supports public broadcasting where it exists through editorial independence safeguards, sustainable funding insulated from political pressure, plurality of voices, multilingual programming, cultural representation, anti-political-instrumentalization, and structural commitment that public broadcasting serves citizens rather than state.

The discipline: editorial independence absolute; funding insulated from political pressure; plurality of voices; multilingual programming; multicultural representation; anti-political-instrumentalization; anti-commercial-encroachment; civil society engagement; civic and educational content prioritized; emergency communications capacity.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Editorial independence absolute.** Constitutional where possible.
2. **Funding insulated from political pressure.**
3. **Plurality of voices.** Across perspectives, demographics, regions.
4. **Multilingual programming.** Tier 1 languages at parity.
5. **Multicultural representation.** Indigenous and minority included.
6. **Anti-political-instrumentalization.** Anti-state-propaganda.
7. **Anti-commercial-encroachment.** Public service mission preserved.
8. **Civil society engagement.** Real, not performative.
9. **Civic and educational content prioritized.** Beyond entertainment.
10. **Emergency communications capacity.** Per Companion 30.

## 2. Public broadcasting role

### 2.1 Functions

- Cultural content (indigenous, minority, regional, national).
- Educational programming.
- Civic information.
- Children's programming (anti-commercial per Companion 37).
- News and current affairs (independent).
- Emergency communications.
- Cross-cultural dialogue.
- Historical preservation.
- Investigative journalism.

### 2.2 Differentiation from commercial media

- Public mission, not commercial.
- Plurality of voices, not target-audience optimization.
- Long-form content possible.
- Anti-targeting-based content.
- Public service obligations.

### 2.3 Discipline

- Anti-commercial-mission-drift.
- Anti-political-instrumentalization.
- Plurality preserved.

## 3. Editorial independence

### 3.1 The principle

Editorial decisions independent of government, commercial, and political pressure.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Constitutional or statutory independence.
- Editor selection through transparent process.
- Anti-political-appointment to editorial positions.
- Multi-year fixed terms with high removal thresholds.
- Editorial board diversity.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Anti-state-direction of editorial content.
- Anti-commercial-direction of editorial content.
- Plurality of editorial voices.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Political control of editorial decisions.
- Commercial pressure determining editorial.
- Use of public broadcasting for state propaganda.
- AI-generated political content from public broadcasting.

---

## 4. Funding model

### 4.1 Mechanisms

- Public funding through dedicated mechanism (license fee, tax allocation, sovereign budget per applicable law).
- Multi-year funding commitments.
- Funding insulated from annual political negotiation where possible.
- Anti-funding-cuts as political punishment.
- Commercial revenue strictly limited where applicable.

### 4.2 Discipline

- Sustainable funding.
- Anti-political-instrumentalization of funding.
- Transparent budget reporting.

### 4.3 Forbidden

- Funding cuts as political punishment.
- Funding tied to specific editorial outcomes.
- Hidden commercial influence through funding.
- Use of funding mechanism to suppress unpopular content.

## 16. The public broadcasting north star

Public broadcasting is civic infrastructure when it functions independently. CivicOS supports public broadcasting through editorial independence safeguards, sustainable funding insulated from political pressure, plurality of voices, multilingual programming, multicultural representation, anti-political-instrumentalization, anti-commercial-encroachment, civil society engagement, civic and educational content priority, and emergency communications capacity.

When public broadcasting becomes a tool of state propaganda, commercial extraction, cultural homogenization, or political-incumbent-instrumentalization — it has failed its public service mission. Capability without public broadcasting discipline is not progress; it is the institutionalization of state media disguised as public service.

When public broadcasting operates with discipline — editorial independence, plurality, sustainable funding, civil society engagement, emergency capacity — it earns the right to be civic infrastructure for plural societies.

The discipline is daily. The editorial independence is constitutional. The funding is insulated. The plurality is real. The civil society engagement is honored.

Public broadcasting where it exists is civic infrastructure. Where it doesn't exist, CivicOS supports plural information ecosystems through other means (per Companions 35, 53, 72, 74). Either way, the commitment is to plural information serving citizens rather than power.

Anything less abandons citizens to information ecosystems that serve power rather than people.
