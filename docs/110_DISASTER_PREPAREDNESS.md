# CivicOS — Disaster Preparedness (Companion 110)

This companion specifies disaster preparedness operations — the routine, non-crisis work that determines whether crises become manageable events or catastrophes. It complements Companion 30 (crisis and emergency operations — focused on active emergencies), Companion 23 (climate twin and risk monitoring), Companion 102 (fragile contexts), and Companion 48 (humanitarian) by focusing specifically on the preparedness work happening before disasters strike.

The thesis: **preparedness is the long quiet work that makes crisis response possible**. Drills, stockpiles, pre-positioned playbooks, capacity building, community engagement, infrastructure resilience, cross-sector coordination — all of this happens in normal times for the benefit of abnormal times. CivicOS supports preparedness systematically.

The discipline: routine drills; updated stockpiles; community-led preparedness; cross-sector coordination; vulnerable population identification; pre-positioned resources; international cooperation; honest assessment of preparedness gaps.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Preparedness is daily.** Not occasional.
2. **Community-led where possible.** Affected communities know best.
3. **Vulnerable populations identified.** Specific preparedness.
4. **Cross-sector coordination.** Disasters cross sectors.
5. **Pre-positioned resources.** Anticipatory action.
6. **Drills routine.** Per Companion 05 §4.3.
7. **International cooperation.** Per planetary protocols.
8. **Cultural and linguistic preparedness.** Multi-modal.
9. **Honest assessment.** Anti-cover-up of gaps.
10. **Continuous improvement.** Learning from drills and real events.

## 2. Disaster classes

| Class | Examples | Preparedness focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural — sudden onset** | Earthquake, flash flood, fire, cyclone, volcanic | Evacuation, immediate response, search and rescue |
| **Natural — slow onset** | Drought, sea level rise, slow flooding | Anticipatory action, adaptation, migration |
| **Climate-coupled** | Heat waves, climate displacement | Climate twin signals, anticipatory action |
| **Public health** | Pandemic, outbreak, mass casualty | Surveillance, stockpiles, response capacity |
| **Cyber** | Major breach, ransomware, infrastructure attack | Cyber resilience, response capacity |
| **Financial** | Bank failure, payment rail disruption, currency crisis | Stabilizers, deposit insurance, communications |
| **Infrastructure** | Grid failure, water failure, transport disruption | Redundancy, restoration capacity |
| **Civil unrest** | Major protests, communal violence | Communication, dialogue, civil liberties protection |
| **Security** | Terror attack, border incident | Response, civil liberties safeguards |
| **Compound** | Multiple concurrent | Cross-sector coordination |

## 4. Pre-positioned playbooks

Per Companion 30 §3.2:

### 4.1 Required

- Per disaster class.
- Per population (urban, rural, indigenous, refugee, etc.).
- Per geography.
- Per cross-sector scenarios.

### 4.2 Playbook contents

- Triggers and thresholds.
- Authority structure.
- Required actions per phase.
- Communications templates (multilingual, accessible).
- Resource needs.
- Cross-agency coordination.
- Civil society engagement.
- International cooperation patterns.

### 4.3 Discipline

- Rehearsed regularly.
- Updated based on drills and real events.
- Public version available where appropriate.

### 4.4 Forbidden

- Playbooks that exist but aren't rehearsed.
- Playbooks not updated for years.
- Playbooks inaccessible during use.

## 5. Drills

Per Companion 05 §4.3:

### 5.1 Cadence

- T0 service failover: monthly.
- Region failover: quarterly.
- Restore-from-backup integrity: quarterly.
- Air-gapped vault restoration: semi-annual.
- Per-disaster-class playbook drill: at least annually.
- Full national tabletop with ministers: annually.
- Cross-sovereign tabletop under treaty: at least annually.
- Civilization-scale tabletop: at least once per protocol family annually.

### 5.2 Discipline

- Drills include adversarial elements.
- Drill failures are diagnostic, not punitive.
- Findings drive remediation with deadlines.

### 5.3 Forbidden

- Symbolic-only drills.
- Suppression of drill failure reports.
- Use of drill findings for political purposes.

## 17. The disaster preparedness north star

Preparedness is the long quiet work that makes crisis response possible. CivicOS supports preparedness through risk assessment, pre-positioned playbooks, drills, stockpiles, anticipatory action, community-led preparedness, vulnerable population identification, cross-sector coordination, communications, infrastructure resilience, public education, and international cooperation.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of preparedness rhetoric without substance, stockpile capture, surveillance through preparedness, or political instrumentalization of disaster response — it has failed at the long work. Capability without preparedness discipline is not progress; it is the institutionalization of crises that should have been manageable.

When the platform supports preparedness with discipline, community engagement, equity, and cross-sector coordination — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies that meet their hardest moments with capacity rather than catastrophe.

The discipline is daily. The drills are regular. The stockpiles are maintained. The community engagement is real. The vulnerable populations are supported. The infrastructure is resilient.

Disasters are not optional. Preparedness response is. The platform supports preparedness for the days when nothing happens and for the days when something does. Anything less abandons citizens to crises that better preparation could have absorbed.
