# CivicOS — Housing and Shelter (Companion 64)

This companion specifies how CivicOS supports housing as constitutional infrastructure. It complements Volume I §15 (CivicLand), §21 (CivicBuild), §12 (CivicCity), and Companion 40 (commons) by going deep on the operational and ethical design of housing systems that serve people, not just markets.

The thesis: **housing is not commodity in the same way other goods are; it is shelter, dignity, foundation for civic life, locus of family and community**. CivicOS supports universal access to adequate shelter through public housing, social housing, anti-displacement, anti-homelessness infrastructure, equitable urban planning, and tenant protections — within sovereign and cultural variation.

The discipline: housing as right (where constitutionally chosen); anti-discrimination strict; anti-displacement structural; tenants protected; vulnerable populations sheltered; indigenous and customary housing systems respected; climate-adaptive housing planning; affordable housing prioritized.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Adequate shelter is foundational.** Without it, civic participation is undermined.
2. **Anti-discrimination strict.** Housing access regardless of race, gender, religion, ability, family status.
3. **Anti-displacement structural.** Forced evictions resisted; due process required.
4. **Tenant protections meaningful.** Beyond paper rights.
5. **Vulnerable populations sheltered.** Homeless, refugees, survivors of violence.
6. **Indigenous and customary housing respected.** Including pastoralist and other non-fixed dwelling.
7. **Climate-adaptive planning.** Housing that survives changing conditions.
8. **Affordable housing prioritized.** Floors before market.
9. **Quality minimum standards.** Health, safety, accessibility.
10. **Community sovereignty in development.** Affected communities have voice.

## 2. Housing systems

### 2.1 Public housing

- State-owned housing for low and moderate income.
- Allocation transparent and equitable.
- Maintenance standards.
- Anti-corruption strict in allocation.
- Resident voice in management.

### 2.2 Social housing

- Cooperative and non-profit housing.
- Subsidies for low-income.
- Anti-speculation protections.

### 2.3 Private rental

- Tenant protections.
- Rent regulation per applicable law.
- Anti-discrimination.
- Habitability standards.
- Eviction due process.

### 2.4 Owner-occupied

- Mortgage access through CivicPay.
- Anti-predatory lending.
- Property registration through CivicLand.
- Anti-foreclosure protections (with bank rights balanced).

### 2.5 Customary and informal

- Customary tenure recognized (Companion 36 §5).
- Informal settlements supported with infrastructure rather than displaced.
- Pastoralist mobility supported.
- Anti-criminalization of homelessness.

## 3. Anti-displacement

### 3.1 The principle

Forced eviction is a deep injustice. Anti-displacement is structural.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Eviction due process.
- Pre-eviction support and alternatives.
- Resettlement when displacement unavoidable (with community input).
- Anti-gentrification protections in vulnerable neighborhoods.
- Indigenous community anti-dispossession (Companion 36).
- Anti-development displacement.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Burden of justification on those seeking displacement.
- Independent review.
- Community participation.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Mass forced eviction.
- Use of platform to facilitate dispossession.
- Algorithmic eviction decisions.
- Discrimination in eviction.

---

## 4. Anti-homelessness

### 4.1 The principle

Homelessness is a failure of housing systems, not a moral failing of homeless people.

### 4.2 Mechanisms

- Housing First approach where chosen.
- Shelter access universal.
- Outreach to street homeless.
- Mental health and substance use support integrated.
- Anti-criminalization of homelessness.
- Identity for homeless persons (provisional CivicID with full dignity per Volume I §10.5).

### 4.3 Discipline

- Trauma-informed.
- Cultural sensitivity.
- Anti-discrimination.
- Anti-surveillance.

### 4.4 Forbidden

- Criminalization of homelessness.
- Surveillance through homelessness services.
- Discrimination in shelter access.
- Use of homelessness data for adverse decisions in other contexts.

---

## 5. Tenant protections

### 5.1 Mechanisms

- Right to safe, habitable housing.
- Right to security from arbitrary eviction.
- Rent regulation per applicable law.
- Anti-discrimination in rental.
- Anti-retaliation for tenant complaints.
- Tenant union recognition.
- Repair and maintenance enforcement.

### 5.2 Discipline

- Complaints accessible (multilingual, multi-modal).
- Inspections honest.
- Anti-corruption in housing inspection.

### 5.3 Forbidden

- Algorithmic discrimination in rental decisions.
- Surveillance of tenant life beyond legitimate purposes.
- Retaliation for tenant organizing.

## 9. Affordable housing

### 9.1 The principle

Affordability is not abstract; it is the share of household income required for adequate housing.

### 9.2 Mechanisms

- Public housing investment.
- Housing subsidies for low-income.
- Inclusionary zoning where applicable.
- Anti-speculation protections.
- Land trusts and community ownership.
- Transit subsidies.

### 9.3 Discipline

- Anti-corruption in affordable housing programs.
- Anti-discrimination.
- Long-term affordability preservation.

### 9.4 Forbidden

- Affordable housing programs that displace existing residents.
- Algorithmic targeting that systematically excludes some groups.
- Commercial capture of affordable housing infrastructure.

- Climate resilience standards.
- Anti-overcrowding standards (with cultural sensitivity).

### 10.2 Enforcement

## 11. Climate-adaptive housing

### 11.1 Mechanisms

- Climate twin coupling with housing planning.
- Heat-resilient design.
- Flood-resilient siting and construction.
- Storm-resilient design.
- Energy-positive housing where feasible.
- Just transition for fossil-fuel-dependent communities.

### 11.2 Discipline

- Climate-displaced supported humanely (Companion 48 §8).
- Long-horizon planning.
- Equity in adaptation support.

### 11.3 Forbidden

- Climate-incompatible long-horizon housing investments.
- Climate adaptation that further displaces vulnerable.
- Algorithmic exclusion from adaptation support.

## 15. The housing north star

A society that does not shelter its people has failed at the basic. Housing is not just commodity; it is foundation for everything else — health, education, employment, family, civic participation, dignity.

CivicOS supports universal access to adequate shelter through public housing, social housing, tenant protections, anti-displacement, anti-homelessness infrastructure, equitable urban planning, indigenous customary housing recognition, climate-adaptive design, and anti-discrimination across all housing systems.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of mass eviction, algorithmic discrimination in housing, surveillance through homelessness services, displacement through "development," or commercial capture of affordable housing — it has failed the foundation of civic life. Capability without housing equity is not progress; it is the institutionalization of homelessness and displacement.

When the platform serves shelter for all — through universal access, anti-discrimination, anti-displacement, tenant protections, vulnerable population support, indigenous and customary respect, climate adaptation — it earns the right to be infrastructure for dignified civic life.

The discipline is daily. The shelter is universal. The protection is structural. The foundation is preserved.

Housing is foundation. The platform exists for the foundation. Anything less abandons the people whose lives the platform was meant to serve.
