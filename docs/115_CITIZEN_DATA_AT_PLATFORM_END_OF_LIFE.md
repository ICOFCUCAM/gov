# CivicOS — Citizen Data at Platform End-of-Life (Companion 115)

This companion specifies what happens to citizen data when a CivicOS deployment ends — through sovereign exit, sunset, replacement, or other forms of platform discontinuation. It complements Companion 20 (sustainability, sunset, exit), Companion 32 (BAI data sovereignty), Companion 38 (health data), Companion 23 (twin lifecycle), Companion 41 (memory and preservation), and Companion 17 (wallet portability) by being specifically about data fate when platforms end.

The thesis: **citizen data must not be orphaned, exploited, or lost when platforms end**. Sovereign exit is a real option (per Companion 20 §7), platforms sunset over decades (per Companion 41), and citizen data spans these transitions. The platform's commitment to citizens includes commitment about what happens to their data when the platform itself ends or transforms.

The discipline: citizen data sovereignty preserved through transitions; portable formats for all citizen-affecting data; deletion options preserved; archival per statutory requirements; anti-commercial-exploitation at end-of-life; civil society oversight; cross-sovereign cooperation where applicable; honest transition planning.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Citizen data sovereignty preserved.** Through any transition.
2. **Portable formats.** All citizen-affecting data exportable.
3. **Deletion options preserved.** Citizen right to deletion.
4. **Archival per statutory requirements.** What must be kept is kept.
5. **Anti-commercial-exploitation at end-of-life.**
6. **Civil society oversight.** Of end-of-life data handling.
7. **Cross-sovereign cooperation.** Where data spans borders.
8. **Honest transition planning.** Citizens informed.
9. **Successor responsibility.** Where successor exists.
10. **Continuity for citizens.** No data orphaned.

---

## 2. End-of-life scenarios

| Scenario | Description | Data fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Sovereign exit** | Sovereign leaves CivicOS for alternative | Migration to alternative platform |
| **Module sunset** | Specific module retired | Data archived or migrated to successor |
| **Vendor change** | Different commercial steward | Data continuity with vendor change |
| **Foundation reformation** | Different governance | Data continuity through governance change |
| **Sovereign deployment dissolution** | State decides to discontinue | Data archived; citizens supported |
| **Cross-sovereign federation dissolution** | Federation ends | Data returns to sovereign control |
| **Catastrophic failure** | Unexpected platform loss | Recovery from backups; archival preserved |

Each scenario has distinct data handling considerations.

## 3. Citizen data sovereignty through transition

### 3.1 The principle

Citizens own their data through any platform transition.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Wallet contents remain citizen-controlled.
- Sealed compartments preserved with citizen keys.
- Civic Data Trusts continue with sovereign-controlled custodians.
- Portable formats per Companion 20 §7 standards.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Anti-citizen-data-as-vendor-asset at transition.
- Anti-commercial-exploitation in transition.
- Citizen agency preserved.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Citizen data treated as vendor asset at transition.
- Sale of citizen data at transition.
- Use of transition as cover for adverse decisions about citizens.

## 9. Archival

### 9.1 What's preserved

Per statutory requirements, typically:
- Civil registration records.
- Property records.
- Tax records (statutory retention period).
- Health records (long retention).
- Judicial records.
- Audit Vault.
- Election records (per electoral law).

### 9.2 What's deleted

Per citizen right and statutory permission:
- Behavioral data beyond retention.
- Wallet contents per citizen choice.
- Sealed compartments per citizen choice.
- Non-essential operational logs.

### 9.3 Mechanisms

- Sovereign-controlled archival.
- Multi-format preservation (Companion 41 §7).
- Long-term preservation per statute.
- Access control preserved.

### 9.4 Discipline

- Anti-discrimination in archival.
- Anti-political-manipulation of historical record.
- Multi-perspective preservation.

### 9.5 Forbidden

- Politicized rewriting of archived records.
- Selective archival favoring narrow interests.
- Commercial exploitation of archives.

## 16. The citizen data at end-of-life north star

Citizen data must not be orphaned, exploited, or lost when platforms end. CivicOS commits to citizen data sovereignty through transitions, portable formats, deletion options preserved, archival per statutory requirements, anti-commercial-exploitation, civil society oversight, cross-sovereign cooperation where applicable, and honest transition planning.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of citizen data commercial exploitation at transition, surprise transitions, weakening of commitments through transition, or politicized rewriting of records — it has failed at the trust commitment that justifies the platform existing in the first place. Capability without end-of-life discipline is not progress; it is the institutionalization of citizen abandonment when platforms become inconvenient.

When the platform supports honest transition with citizen sovereignty preserved, portable data, deletion options, archival per law, and constitutional officer engagement — it earns the trust that comes from knowing citizens are served throughout the platform's lifecycle and even at its end.

The discipline is daily even in the rare transition moments. The data sovereignty is structural. The portability is real. The civil society engagement is honored. The constitutional officer access is preserved.

Platforms end. Citizen relationships with their data must outlast platforms. The platform's commitment to citizens includes commitment about what happens when the platform itself ends. Anything less abandons citizens at the moment when transition vulnerability is highest.
