# CivicOS — Sport, Recreation, Leisure, and Wellbeing (Companion 89)

This companion goes deep on sport, recreation, leisure, and wellbeing infrastructure beyond what Companion 53 §5 (sport in civic life) covers. It complements Companion 38 (health), Companion 46 (disability), Companion 47 (gender), Companion 37 (children), and Companion 64 (housing) by treating sport, recreation, leisure, and wellbeing as serious civic infrastructure rather than peripheral concerns.

The thesis: **a society that does not support its citizens' sport, recreation, leisure, and wellbeing has misunderstood what civic life is for**. Sport builds health, community, and dignity. Recreation supports child development, family bonds, and cross-generational connection. Leisure is the time in which civic life happens beyond compulsion. Wellbeing — physical, mental, social, spiritual — is the actual purpose of much of what governments do. CivicOS supports these dimensions through equitable sport infrastructure, accessible public spaces for recreation, anti-discrimination in participation, indigenous and minority sport recognition, anti-doping with civil liberties, and infrastructure for the time citizens have for living rather than working.

The discipline: equitable access across gender, ability, region, income, age; indigenous and minority sport recognized; anti-doping with civil liberties safeguards; child athlete protection; women's sport investment; disability sport supported; mass participation prioritized over elite-only investment; anti-commercial-extraction; recreation infrastructure equitably distributed.

### 5.3 Discipline

## 6. Anti-doping

### 6.1 The pattern

Anti-doping in sport involves testing and sanctions. Civil liberties safeguards required.

### 6.2 Mechanisms

- Testing per international standards (WADA-aligned where applicable).
- Athlete consent within sport participation.
- Due process in sanctions.
- Appeal mechanisms.
- Privacy in testing.

### 6.3 Discipline

- Anti-discriminatory testing.
- Athlete privacy beyond testing purpose.
- Anti-criminalization of recreational use disguised as sport policy.

### 6.4 Forbidden

- Surveillance disguised as anti-doping.
- Discriminatory testing patterns.
- Use of anti-doping data for non-sport adverse decisions.
- Cross-border anti-doping cooperation exposing athletes to harm.

## 17. The sport, recreation, leisure, wellbeing north star

A society that supports its citizens' sport, recreation, leisure, and wellbeing has understood what civic life is for. CivicOS supports these dimensions through equitable sport infrastructure, accessible recreation, anti-discrimination, indigenous and minority recognition, child protection, women's sport investment, disability sport, mass participation prioritization, anti-commercial-extraction, and right to rest.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of sport elite capture, child athlete exploitation, anti-doping surveillance, indigenous sport appropriation, recreation privatization, wellbeing instrumentalization, or rest erosion — it has failed civic life beyond mere governance. Capability without recognition of citizens' wellbeing is not progress; it is the reduction of civic life to compulsion.

When the platform supports flourishing — through sport, recreation, leisure, and wellbeing infrastructure with equity, dignity, and respect — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies people actually want to live in.

The discipline is daily. The equity is structural. The protection is real. The cultural respect is honored. The right to rest is preserved.

Civic life exceeds politics. The platform supports the dimensions where citizens live, play, rest, and flourish. Anything less reduces civic infrastructure to bureaucracy and abandons what makes life worth governing for.
