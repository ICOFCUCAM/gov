# CivicOS — Translation Operations (Companion 129)

This companion specifies the operational practice of translation across CivicOS — how translation happens day-to-day to maintain Tier 1 parity across all national languages, with native review, cultural appropriateness, and continuous quality monitoring. It complements Companion 25 (multilingual and cultural overview), Companion 12 §9 (voice and language strategy), Companion 36 (indigenous language sovereignty), and Companion 22 §6 (plain language) by being specifically about translation operations.

The thesis: **translation is operational discipline, not occasional task**. Maintaining Tier 1 parity across all national languages requires sustained operational capacity — professional translators, native speakers, continuous quality monitoring, translation memory infrastructure, cultural review, and integration with software development cycles. Done badly, translation produces second-class service for non-dominant-language speakers.

The discipline: professional translators; native speaker review; continuous quality monitoring; cultural appropriateness; translation memory shared; sovereign-controlled translation infrastructure; integration with development; multilingual at parity; anti-machine-translation in production for consequential content; civil society engagement on translation quality.

## 17. The translation operations north star

Translation is operational discipline, not occasional task. CivicOS supports translation through professional translators, native speaker review, continuous quality monitoring, cultural appropriateness, shared translation memory, sovereign-controlled infrastructure, integration with development, multilingual parity, anti-MT-in-production for consequential content, civil society engagement, and indigenous community sovereignty.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of language inequality, translation memory extraction, MT-as-production-shortcut, or cultural-imposition-through-translation — it has failed at multilingual parity. Capability without translation discipline is not progress; it is the institutionalization of second-class service for non-dominant-language speakers.

When the platform supports translation operations with discipline, professional translators, cultural appropriateness, and quality monitoring — it earns the right to be infrastructure for genuinely multilingual societies.

The discipline is daily. The translators are professional. The native review is unconditional. The cultural appropriateness is structural. The community sovereignty is respected. The continuous quality monitoring is real.

Translation makes the platform multilingual or it doesn't. Done well, citizens experience platform in their language equivalent to dominant-language speakers. Done badly, the platform is monolingual with translated wrapper.

Anything less abandons non-dominant-language speakers to second-class service through translation neglect.
