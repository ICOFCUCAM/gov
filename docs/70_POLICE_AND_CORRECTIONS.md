# CivicOS — Police, Prisons, and Corrections (Companion 70)

This companion specifies how CivicOS treats the most contested area of state-citizen relations: policing, pretrial detention, prisons, probation, parole, and post-release support. It complements Volume I §19 (CivicGuard) and §20 (CivicJustice), Companion 24 (security/rights balance), Companion 30 (crisis operations), and Companion 49 (peacebuilding) by going deep on the operational and ethical design of carceral and policing systems.

The thesis: **policing and corrections are where state coercive power meets citizens most directly, and where digital infrastructure is most easily turned against citizens**. CivicOS approaches this domain with extreme caution: structural anti-surveillance, anti-racial-and-religious-targeting, due process safeguards, anti-mass-incarceration, alternatives to incarceration, anti-recidivism through rehabilitation, dignity in detention, restorative approaches where chosen, and sovereign discretion respected within constitutional limits.

The discipline: do no harm; police and prison data heavily restricted; algorithmic decisions in policing and corrections strictly limited; no predictive policing of individuals; no algorithmic risk-scoring affecting liberty; rehabilitation prioritized; community-based alternatives supported; due process strict at every stage; civil liberties safeguards enhanced; international standards (Mandela Rules, Bangkok Rules, Tokyo Rules, Beijing Rules) reflected in operations.

This is necessarily a companion where the *forbidden list* is long and central. The corpus's commitment to civil liberties is most acute here.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Coercive power most contained.** Where state coerces citizens, structural safeguards strictest.
2. **Due process strict at every stage.** No shortcuts.
3. **Anti-mass-incarceration.** Detention as last resort.
4. **Rehabilitation prioritized.** Most people released; what they return to matters.
5. **Anti-racial-and-religious-targeting.** Disparate impact monitored and remediated.
6. **Anti-predictive-policing of individuals.** Forbidden absolutely.
7. **Anti-algorithmic-risk-scoring affecting liberty.** Forbidden.
8. **Dignity in detention.** Detained persons retain rights other than liberty.
9. **Children's specific protections.** Minors treated separately and developmentally.
10. **Community-based alternatives supported.** Where carceral is not necessary.

## 2. Policing infrastructure

### 2.1 What CivicGuard supports

- Computer-aided dispatch.
- Incident reporting.
- Officer scheduling.
- Evidence management with chain of custody.
- Accountability infrastructure (body camera, complaint, internal affairs).
- Cross-agency coordination.
- Communication with citizens.

### 2.2 What CivicGuard does not support

- Predictive policing of individuals.
- Algorithmic risk-scoring of citizens for adverse police attention.
- Mass surveillance.
- Facial recognition of crowds.
- Identity-based watch lists.
- Use of platform for political opposition surveillance.
- Use of platform for ethnic, religious, regional persecution.

These are forbidden absolutely (Companion 24 §2).

### 2.3 Officer accountability

- Officer interactions with citizens recorded (within privacy frameworks).
- Body camera footage preserved per policy.
- Use-of-force incidents reviewed independently.
- Civilian oversight bodies with authority and access.
- Whistleblower protection for officers reporting misconduct.
- Discipline transparent.

### 2.4 Discipline

- Police discretion exists; documented.
- Disparate impact monitored continuously.
- Anti-discrimination strict.
- Algorithmic decision support audited (Class B advisory only; never determinative).

### 2.5 Forbidden

- Algorithmic policing decisions absent human judgment.
- Surveillance of journalism or civil society in lawful work.
- Chilling effect surveillance of protests.
- Use of platform to suppress dissent.

## 3. Pretrial detention

### 3.1 The principle

Pretrial detention is a deep deprivation of liberty for people not yet convicted. Strict scrutiny.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Bail/release decisions through judicial process.
- Algorithmic risk-scoring forbidden (Class D, never determinative).
- Time-bounded pretrial detention.
- Right to counsel.
- Pretrial services for community-based supervision.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Detention as last resort.
- Equity in pretrial decisions monitored (race, class, gender disparities surfaced).
- Anti-corruption in bail-bond systems where they exist.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Algorithmic pretrial detention decisions.
- Money bail discriminating against poor.
- Indefinite pretrial detention.
- Use of pretrial detention as coercion to plead.

---

## 4. Prisons and detention

### 4.1 The principle

Detention deprives liberty; it does not deprive other rights. Detained persons retain dignity, access to family, healthcare, education, contact with outside world.

### 4.2 Standards

- Mandela Rules (UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners).
- Bangkok Rules (women).
- Tokyo Rules (non-custodial measures).
- Beijing Rules (juvenile justice).
- Specific national constitutional and statutory protections.

### 4.3 Mechanisms

- Detention facility management (not prison-as-product; prison-as-managed-rights-context).
- Healthcare access through CivicHealth.
- Education access through CivicLearn.
- Family contact infrastructure.
- Legal aid access through CivicJustice.
- Mental health support.
- Substance use support where applicable.
- Vocational training.
- Religious accommodation per Companion 51.

### 4.4 Discipline

- Anti-overcrowding.
- Anti-violence (between detainees, by staff).
- Anti-corruption in detention administration.
- Independent inspection.
- Family and civil society visit access.
- Anti-discrimination strict.
- Cultural accommodation.

### 4.5 Forbidden

- Use of detention for political punishment.
- Algorithmic decisions on detention conditions absent human judgment.
- Surveillance of detained beyond legitimate security needs.
- Use of detention data for adverse decisions on family.
- Commercial exploitation of detained labor without rights.
- Algorithmic discipline determinations.
- Solitary confinement beyond strict limits.
- Detention of children with adults.
- Detention of asylum-seekers absent strict necessity.
- Detention of migrants in default.

## 5. Children in conflict with law

Per Beijing Rules and CRC:

### 5.1 The principle

Children in conflict with law are children first. Best interests principle paramount.

### 5.2 Mechanisms

- Specialized juvenile justice systems.
- Diversion from formal justice where appropriate.
- Detention as truly last resort, shortest period.
- Education continued during detention.
- Family contact preserved.
- Mental health support.
- Reintegration support.
- Confidentiality of records.

### 5.3 Discipline

- Trauma-informed.
- Cultural and developmental sensitivity.
- Age-appropriate process.

### 5.4 Forbidden

- Detention of children with adults.
- Algorithmic risk-scoring of juvenile defendants.
- Use of juvenile records for adult adverse decisions.
- Capital punishment of children (international standard).
- Life sentences for children (international standard).

## 6. Probation and parole

### 6.1 The principle

Community-based supervision as alternative to detention; supports reintegration.

### 6.2 Mechanisms

- Supervision through community-based officers.
- Conditions of supervision proportionate.
- Resources for compliance (housing, employment, treatment).
- Anti-technical-violation revocation (sending people back for minor non-criminal violations).
- Reintegration support.

### 6.3 Discipline

- Anti-discrimination in conditions.
- Algorithmic risk-scoring forbidden for revocation decisions.
- Civil liberties preserved in supervision.

### 6.4 Forbidden

- Algorithmic revocation decisions absent human judgment.
- Surveillance through supervision beyond legitimate purposes.
- Use of supervision data for adverse decisions in unrelated contexts.
- Conditions impossible to comply with by design.

---

## 7. Reentry and post-release

### 7.1 The principle

Most detained persons return to community. What they return to determines recidivism.

### 7.2 Mechanisms

- Identity restoration (CivicID and Civic Wallet).
- Housing support (per Companion 64).
- Employment support (per CivicWork).
- Mental health and substance use treatment continuity.
- Family reunification support.
- Legal aid for civil matters arising from incarceration.
- Voting rights restoration per applicable law.
- Anti-discrimination in employment, housing, services.

### 7.3 Discipline

- "Banning the box" or equivalent anti-discrimination.
- Algorithmic discrimination against formerly incarcerated audited.
- Trauma-informed support.

### 7.4 Forbidden

- Permanent civil disability for past conviction (where constitutionally limited).
- Algorithmic blacklisting of formerly incarcerated.
- Surveillance of formerly incarcerated beyond legitimate limited purposes.
- Use of formerly-incarcerated status for unrelated adverse decisions.

## 8. Restorative justice

### 8.1 The principle

Where chosen by victims and communities, restorative approaches offer alternative to purely punitive responses.

### 8.2 Mechanisms

- Restorative conferences with informed consent of all parties.
- Victim-offender dialogue infrastructure.
- Community-based restoration.
- Indigenous and community justice frameworks recognized (per Companion 36).

### 8.3 Discipline

- Voluntary participation.
- Adequate support for victims.
- Cultural appropriateness.
- Constitutional rights preserved.

### 8.4 Forbidden

- Coerced restorative process.
- Restorative process as cover for impunity.
- Use of restorative data for surveillance.

## 9. Decriminalization domains

### 9.1 The principle

Some conduct that was historically criminalized is increasingly recognized as not requiring criminal response: substance use, sex work in some contexts, homelessness, mental health crisis, vagrancy, etc. CivicOS supports sovereign movement toward proportionate responses.

### 9.2 Mechanisms

- Health-based responses to substance use where chosen.
- Public health approach to sex work where chosen.
- Anti-criminalization of homelessness (Companion 64 §4).
- Mental health crisis response with mental health professionals, not police.
- Diversion from criminal justice to appropriate services.

### 9.3 Discipline

- Sovereign discretion respected.
- Anti-discrimination in enforcement of remaining criminal law.
- Trauma-informed responses.

### 9.4 Forbidden

- Algorithmic enforcement of decriminalized conduct.
- Surveillance through health-based responses.
- Discrimination in service provision.

## 10. Sentencing

### 10.1 The principle

Sentencing is fundamentally human judgment. Algorithmic determinism is forbidden.

### 10.2 Mechanisms

- Sentencing through judicial process.
- Sentencing guidelines transparent.
- Mandatory minimums avoided where possible (they remove judicial discretion).
- Individualized assessment.
- Mitigation considered.

### 10.3 Discipline

- Anti-discrimination in sentencing.
- Disparate impact monitored.
- Algorithmic Ombudsman scrutiny of sentencing patterns.

### 10.4 Forbidden

- Algorithmic sentencing decisions (Class D forbidden).
- Algorithmic risk-scoring used in sentencing.
- Sentencing based on protected characteristics.

---

## 11. Death penalty

### 11.1 The principle

CivicOS does not include capabilities for death-penalty administration. Where sovereigns retain death penalty, they administer through their own systems with their own moral and constitutional weight.

### 11.2 What CivicOS will not do

- Provide algorithmic decision support for death penalty cases.
- Provide automated execution-related capabilities.
- Integrate with death penalty administration in ways that automate.

### 11.3 What CivicOS supports (where lawful)

- Court case management for capital cases.
- Defendant identity verification.
- Appeals processing.

### 11.4 The corpus's view

The corpus does not endorse the death penalty. Where sovereigns choose to retain it, the corpus articulates that the platform will not enable algorithmic determinism in such cases. The moral weight remains entirely with the sovereign.

## 12. Anti-recidivism

### 12.1 The principle

Most people released from detention return to it absent appropriate support. Anti-recidivism is structural.

### 12.2 Mechanisms

- Reentry support (§7).
- Trauma and mental health support.
- Substance use treatment continuity.
- Education and vocational training during detention.
- Community connection during detention (anti-isolation).
- Family preservation.

### 12.3 Discipline

- Outcome metrics tracked.
- Support resources adequate.
- Non-punitive supervision.

## 13. Civilian oversight

### 13.1 The principle

Police and corrections require strong civilian oversight.

### 13.2 Mechanisms

- Independent civilian review boards with authority.
- Inspector General with subpoena authority.
- Algorithmic Ombudsman scrutiny of policing and corrections AI.
- Sovereign Trust Officer for invariant violations.
- Civil society standing.
- Press freedom in covering policing and corrections.
- Whistleblower protection for officers reporting misconduct.

### 13.3 Discipline

- Independent investigation of misconduct.
- Discipline transparent.
- Patterns surfaced and addressed.

### 13.4 Forbidden

- Self-investigation only.
- Suppression of whistleblowers.
- Suppression of journalism on policing.
- Civil society standing weakened.

## 18. The police and corrections north star

Where the state coerces citizens, the structural safeguards must be strictest. CivicOS approaches policing and corrections with extreme caution: no predictive policing of individuals, no algorithmic risk-scoring affecting liberty, no algorithmic sentencing, no automated revocation, no mass surveillance, no detention as default, no commercial exploitation of detention, no algorithmic blacklisting of formerly incarcerated, no surveillance of protest, no identity-based targeting.

What CivicOS supports: due process at every stage, dignity in detention, rehabilitation prioritized, alternatives to incarceration, restorative approaches where chosen, civilian oversight strong, cross-border cooperation respecting human rights, international standards (Mandela, Bangkok, Tokyo, Beijing Rules) reflected, decriminalization where sovereign chooses, anti-discrimination strict.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of mass incarceration, predictive policing, algorithmic determinism in liberty decisions, surveillance of protest, identity-based targeting, or commercial exploitation of detention — it has failed at the most acute test of civil liberties. Capability without civil liberties safeguards in the carceral domain is not progress; it is the institutionalization of mass injustice.

When the platform serves justice — through due process, rehabilitation, alternatives to incarceration, dignity in detention, anti-discrimination, civilian oversight, and refusal of forbidden capabilities — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies pursuing accountable justice.

The discipline is daily. The forbidden list is long for good reason. The protection of liberty is structural. The dignity of detained persons is preserved.

This is where the state's coercive power meets citizens most directly. The platform's commitments are most acute here. Anything less abandons the people most vulnerable to state power.
