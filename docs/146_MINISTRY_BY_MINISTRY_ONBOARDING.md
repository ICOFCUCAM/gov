# CivicOS — Ministry-by-Ministry Onboarding Strategies (Companion 146)

This companion specifies, ministry by ministry, the operational onboarding patterns for sovereign deployments of CivicOS — the engagement model, the legacy audit, the module integration sequence, the data dictionary work, the inclusion-floor verification, the constitutional officer touchpoints, the civil society engagement, the staff training, the operational handover, and the steady-state cadence for each ministry that typically participates in a CivicOS deployment. It complements Companion 04 (deployment and infrastructure), Companion 144 (Africa-first playbook), Companion 145 (regional deployment playbooks), and the country playbook discipline in the Master Blueprint deployment chapters by being specifically about ministry-level onboarding.

The thesis: **a CivicOS deployment is not a single project — it is a federation of ministry-level digital transformations bound by a common kernel, common identity, common audit, and common doctrine — and each ministry onboards on its own timeline with its own discipline**. Imposing a single timeline across ministries fails; allowing each ministry to drift fails differently. CivicOS therefore prescribes ministry-by-ministry onboarding strategies: a clear engagement pattern per ministry, with shared infrastructure prerequisites, with sovereign-specified prioritization, with civil society and constitutional officer engagement, and with measurement of citizen impact.

The discipline: ministry-by-ministry onboarding with shared kernel; sovereign-specified prioritization; legacy audit before integration; data dictionary harmonization; inclusion floor verification per service; constitutional officer touchpoints; civil society engagement; staff training; operational handover; steady-state cadence; replaceability preserved; anti-disruption-of-citizen-service throughout.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Ministry is principal.** Within sovereign, each ministry runs its own onboarding.
2. **Shared kernel.** Identity, wallet, audit, bus, KMS shared.
3. **Sovereign prioritization.** Sovereign decides ministry sequence.
4. **Legacy audit first.** Before integration.
5. **Data dictionary harmonization.** Per Companion 07.
6. **Inclusion floor verification.** Per service, per ministry.
7. **Constitutional officer touchpoints.** Per ministry per phase.
8. **Civil society engagement.** Per ministry per scope.
9. **Staff training.** Anti-deskilling.
10. **Anti-disruption-of-citizen-service.** Throughout.

## 2. Shared prerequisites

Before any ministry onboards services, shared infrastructure must operate:

- **Identity kernel** (Companion 03).
- **Citizen wallet** (Companion 03) with USSD/IVR/agent/walk-in floor.
- **CivicPay** (Companion 71) where payment relevant.
- **Audit Vault** (Companion 06) sovereign-controlled.
- **CivicBus** (Companion 05) interconnect operational.
- **KMS** sovereign-held.
- **Data Dictionary** (Companion 07) seeded.

Per Companion 144 §3.2 and the country playbook, these prerequisites are Phase 1 work. Ministry-by-ministry onboarding begins in Phase 2.

## 3. Generic ministry onboarding pattern

Each ministry follows this generic pattern, adapted to its specifics.

### 3.1 Stage A: Engagement (months 0-3)

- Sovereign Foundation engages ministry leadership.
- Civil society engagement on the ministry's mandate.
- Constitutional officer briefing.
- Legacy system audit (Companion 04 §10).
- Citizen-service inventory.
- Pain-point identification with citizens and civil society.
- Staff consultation (anti-deskilling and anti-job-loss-by-stealth).
- Sovereign decision: proceed, modify, decline.

### 3.2 Stage B: Foundation (months 3-9)

- Ministry data dictionary work (Companion 07).
- Integration with shared kernel.
- Module charter per Companion 138 where AI involved.
- Constitutional officer registration.
- Civil society standing access established.
- Staff training program designed.
- Inclusion-floor design verification per service.
- Pilot population selection (small, representative, with consent).

### 3.3 Stage C: Pilot (months 9-15)

- Pilot operation with small population.
- Parallel operation with legacy.
- Receipts to citizens.
- Contestation channels active.
- Civil society audit access.
- Constitutional officer observation.
- KPIs measured.
- Citizen feedback channels.
- Drift detection.

### 3.4 Stage D: Expansion (months 15-30)

- Pilot expanded geographically and demographically.
- Continued parallel operation with legacy.
- Per-region performance reporting.
- Inclusion floor verification under expansion.
- Civil society and constitutional officer findings integrated.
- KPIs against pre-deployment baseline.

### 3.5 Stage E: Steady state (year 2+)

- Legacy retirement per published timeline.
- Steady-state operations.
- Annual constitutional officer review.
- Annual civil society review.
- Annual Sovereignty Index contribution.
- Generational documentation per Companion 25.

### 3.6 Across all stages

- Manual fallback always (per Companion 27).
- Citizen contestability (per Companion 35).
- Plain language and multilingual (per Companion 22).
- Accessibility (per Companion 110).
- Anti-discrimination across protected attributes.

2. Identity card issuance (with selective disclosure per Companion 03).
3. Residence and travel documentation.
4. Naturalization processes.

- **Anti-discrimination**: per protected attributes.

### 4.6 Forbidden

## 23. The ministry-by-ministry onboarding north star

A CivicOS deployment is a federation of ministry-level digital transformations bound by a common kernel and a common doctrine. Each ministry onboards on its own timeline with its own discipline; each ministry's onboarding respects civil society engagement, constitutional officer oversight, inclusion floor verification, staff training, replaceability, and the seven invariants. Sovereign prioritization decides sequence; doctrine binds the substance.

When CivicOS becomes a deployment where ministries onboard without civil society engagement, without constitutional officer touchpoints, without inclusion floor verification, with staff displaced by stealth, with restricted domains creeping into automation, with cross-ministry data merging beyond per-case consent, with manual fallback retired prematurely — it has failed at the federation discipline. Capability without ministry-by-ministry discipline is the institutionalization of digital administration that erodes citizen relationship while expanding administrative reach.

When the platform supports ministry onboardings that engage civil society from the start, that brief constitutional officers continuously, that verify inclusion floor under expansion, that train staff with anti-deskilling, that preserve replaceability, that honor manual fallback, and that respect the seven invariants — it earns the right to be infrastructure that genuinely digitizes the public sector while keeping democratic discipline.

The discipline is daily. The ministries are principals. The shared kernel is foundation. The civil society is active. The constitutional officers are present. The staff are partners. The citizens are served. The invariants bind.

Ministry-by-ministry onboarding is the operational reality of CivicOS deployment. The platform either does it with discipline, or it does not deploy. Anything less is a single-vendor product imposing a single template on sovereign ministries that deserved to be principals — and the platform's job is to refuse that imposition by design.
