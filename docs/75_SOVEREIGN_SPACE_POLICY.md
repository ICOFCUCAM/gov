# CivicOS — Sovereign Space Policy and Satellite Infrastructure (Companion 75)

This companion specifies how CivicOS treats space-based infrastructure — sovereign satellites, communications, navigation, Earth observation, space governance. It complements Companion 11 §5.2 (satellite evolution), Companion 23 §9 (climate twin with satellite data), Companion 56 (sovereign R&D), Companion 15 (planetary protocols), and Companion 30 (crisis operations) by going deep on the operational and ethical design of space-related platform capabilities.

The thesis: **space infrastructure is increasingly essential to civic functioning — communications, navigation, climate monitoring, agricultural support, disaster response, financial market timing — and dependence on foreign space infrastructure is sovereignty risk**. CivicOS supports sovereign space capability where feasible (own or under sovereign-controlled consortium), responsible space governance, dual-use discipline (civilian-military separation), space debris mitigation, and cross-sovereign cooperation on space commons under planetary protocols.

The discipline: civilian and military space functions separated; space data shared as commons where appropriate; sovereign space capability built progressively; cross-sovereign cooperation under treaty respecting sovereignty; anti-weaponization commitments; space debris mitigation; equitable access to space benefits; indigenous and community involvement in earth observation affecting them.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Space infrastructure is sovereign infrastructure.** Critical to many civic functions.
2. **Civilian and military space separated.** Different governance, different capabilities.
3. **Space data commons where appropriate.** Climate, environment, disaster benefit shared.
4. **Anti-weaponization of space.** Outer Space Treaty principles upheld.
5. **Space debris mitigation.** Long-horizon stewardship.
6. **Cross-sovereign cooperation respects sovereignty.** Treaty-based.
7. **Equitable access to space benefits.** Not just spacefaring nations.
8. **Indigenous and community involvement in EO affecting them.** FPIC where applicable.
9. **Sovereign communications resilience.** Multi-orbit, multi-band.
10. **PNT (positioning, navigation, timing) sovereignty.** Reduce dependence on single source.

## 3. Sovereign communications satellites

### 3.1 The principle

Communications resilience requires sovereign options or sovereign-controlled consortium options.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Sovereign LEO/GEO communications satellites (where feasible).
- Consortium arrangements with sovereign control.
- Multi-orbit, multi-band, multi-vendor.
- Anti-jamming.
- Civilian and military separation.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Sovereign control of routing and security.
- Anti-foreign-coercion of communications.
- Civilian access prioritized.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Sovereign communications satellite use for civilian surveillance.
- Foreign coercion compromising sovereign communications.
- Use for political opposition surveillance.

## 4. PNT (Positioning, Navigation, Timing)

### 4.1 The principle

PNT is critical infrastructure. Sole dependence on any single foreign system is sovereignty risk.

### 4.2 Mechanisms

- Multi-system PNT (GPS, Galileo, BeiDou, GLONASS, regional systems).
- Sovereign PNT augmentation where feasible.
- Anti-spoofing and anti-jamming.
- Resilient timing for financial markets and critical infrastructure.

### 4.3 Discipline

- Sovereign PNT capability progressively built.
- Anti-foreign-coercion of PNT.

### 4.4 Forbidden

- PNT capabilities used for civilian tracking surveillance.
- Foreign coercion compromising PNT.

## 5. Earth observation

### 5.1 The pattern

Earth observation satellites provide climate, environment, agriculture, disaster, and security data.

### 5.2 Mechanisms

- Sovereign EO satellites (where feasible).
- Consortium arrangements.
- Commercial data purchases under sovereign-acceptable terms.
- Open EO data commons (per Companion 35 §4) where appropriate.
- Integration with national twin (Companion 23).

### 5.3 Civilian EO uses

- Climate monitoring.
- Environmental change detection.
- Deforestation alerts.
- Agricultural support.
- Disaster response.
- Cadastral and land use (per Companion 09 — CivicLand).
- Maritime trade tracking.

### 5.4 Indigenous and community EO

Per Companion 36: EO of indigenous territories requires FPIC. Community involvement in design and use.

### 5.5 Discipline

- Civilian and military EO separated.
- Privacy floors (no individual surveillance through EO).
- Indigenous community sovereignty over EO of their territories.

### 5.6 Forbidden

- EO used for civilian individual surveillance.
- EO of indigenous territories without FPIC.
- Commercial exploitation of EO data of communities.

## 6. Space debris and orbital governance

### 6.1 The principle

Low-earth orbit is a commons under stress from satellite proliferation. Stewardship matters.

### 6.2 Mechanisms

- Debris mitigation in satellite design (deorbit at end-of-life).
- Tracking and avoidance.
- Cross-sovereign coordination on orbital safety.
- Standards participation (UN COPUOS, etc.).

### 6.3 Discipline

- Long-horizon stewardship.
- Anti-debris-creating practices.
- International cooperation.

### 6.4 Forbidden

- Anti-satellite weapons creating debris.
- Mega-constellation deployment without debris discipline.

## 7. Anti-weaponization

### 7.1 The principle

Outer Space Treaty (1967) prohibits weapons of mass destruction in orbit. CivicOS supports broader anti-weaponization.

### 7.2 Mechanisms

- No CivicOS support for offensive space capabilities.
- Civilian-military separation in space infrastructure.
- Sovereign space defense (where chosen) separately governed.
- International treaty compliance.

### 7.3 Discipline

- Anti-arms-race in space.
- Multilateral cooperation on space security.

### 7.4 Forbidden

- Use of CivicOS for offensive space operations.
- Civilian space infrastructure repurposed for weapons.
- Anti-satellite weapons.

## 9. Equitable access to space benefits

### 9.1 The principle

Space benefits — climate data, navigation, communications, disaster support — should reach all sovereigns and communities, not just spacefaring nations.

### 9.2 Mechanisms

- Open EO data commons.
- Cross-sovereign capacity building.
- Affordable communications access.
- Disaster data sharing under planetary protocols.
- Climate data shared globally.

### 9.3 Discipline

- Anti-monopolization of space benefits.
- Equity in access.
- Capacity building for non-spacefaring sovereigns.

### 9.4 Forbidden

- Discrimination in space data access.

## 12. Sovereign space capability development

### 12.1 Phased approach

- **Phase 1**: Customer of commercial and consortium space data.
- **Phase 2**: Participant in cross-sovereign consortia.
- **Phase 3**: Sovereign EO and communications satellites.
- **Phase 4**: Sovereign PNT augmentation.
- **Phase 5**: Full sovereign space capability where strategically valued.

Most sovereigns will not progress to Phase 5; that's appropriate. Capability commensurate with needs.

### 12.2 Mechanisms

- Civic Academy with space engineering tracks.
- University partnerships.
- International cooperation in capability building.
- Civic-tech ecosystem for space applications.

### 12.3 Discipline

- Sovereign cost-benefit analysis honest.
- Anti-vanity-spending.
- Equity considerations in sovereign space investments.

### 12.4 Forbidden

- Space capability built at expense of basic citizen needs.
- Vanity space programs displacing essential investments.

## 13. Forbidden in sovereign space

CivicOS will not:

- Permit sovereign space infrastructure use for civilian surveillance.
- Allow EO used for civilian individual surveillance.
- Permit EO of indigenous territories without FPIC.
- Allow commercial exploitation of EO data of communities.
- Permit anti-satellite weapons creating debris.
- Allow mega-constellation deployment without debris discipline.
- Permit use of CivicOS for offensive space operations.
- Allow civilian space infrastructure repurposed for weapons.
- Permit cross-sovereign space cooperation compromising sovereign authority.
- Allow extraction from low-resource sovereigns through space cooperation.
- Permit use of space monopoly to extract from non-spacefaring sovereigns.
- Allow surveillance of indigenous communities through space.
- Permit climate data trade discrimination beyond multilaterally agreed.
- Allow space capability built at expense of basic citizen needs.

This list grows; it does not shrink.

## 15. The sovereign space north star

Space is increasingly essential to civilian life — communications, navigation, climate, agriculture, disaster response. Dependence on foreign space infrastructure is sovereignty risk. CivicOS supports sovereign space capability where feasible (own or under sovereign-controlled consortium), responsible space governance, civilian-military separation, debris mitigation, anti-weaponization, and cross-sovereign cooperation under sovereignty-respecting frameworks.

When CivicOS becomes complicit in civilian surveillance through space, weaponization, debris-creating practices, indigenous territory surveillance, or extractive space monopolies — it has failed responsible space stewardship. Capability without space discipline is not progress; it is the militarization of an increasingly critical commons.

When the platform supports responsible sovereign space capability — through civilian focus, cooperation, equity, anti-weaponization, and indigenous community partnership — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies that participate in space responsibly.

The discipline is daily. The civilian-military separation is structural. The international cooperation is sovereign-respecting. The indigenous community sovereignty is real. The long-horizon stewardship is generational.

Space is humanity's commons under stress. The platform's role is to support sovereign capability and responsible governance, not to militarize, surveil, or monopolize. Anything less abandons the long-horizon commons that future generations will inherit.
