# CivicOS — Brain-Adjacent Interface Ethics and Governance (Companion 32)

This companion specifies how CivicOS approaches brain-adjacent interfaces (BAI) — assistive interfaces that read or write neural signals — across eras. It complements Volume II Part 5 (2050+) and Companion 17 (Civic Wallet evolution) by going deep on the *ethical*, *governance*, and *technical* design that this technology requires.

The thesis: **brain-adjacent interfaces are the most personal information frontier humanity has ever crossed**. Their assistive use for severe disability is transformative and just. Their broader deployment risks are extraordinary: erosion of mental privacy, manipulation of cognition, surveillance of thought, commercial extraction of internal life. CivicOS supports the assistive use unconditionally and constrains the broader use absolutely.

The discipline: BAI in CivicOS is **citizen-owned, sovereign-controlled, opt-in only, narrowly purposed, no commercial pathway, and bound by the strictest privacy regime the platform supports**. Neural data is never subpoenaed except under judicial process; never sold; never aggregated commercially; never used for state surveillance.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Assistive use is a right.** Citizens with severe disability have access to BAI on equal terms.
2. **Opt-in only.** No BAI deployment without explicit, informed, revocable consent.
3. **Citizen owns neural data absolutely.** Stored under citizen-held keys; the state cannot read it.
4. **No commercial pathway.** Neural data has no market in CivicOS jurisdictions.
5. **No state surveillance through BAI.** Neural data is not a tool of governance.
6. **Cognitive liberty.** No state intervention that influences cognition without explicit informed consent.
7. **Mental privacy.** Inner experience is private; the platform respects this absolutely.
8. **Sovereignty over BAI supply chain.** Critical BAI capabilities not foreign-coercible.
9. **Tightly regulated for non-assistive use.** Default-deny; specific permissions for narrow use cases.
10. **Sunset and revocability.** Citizens may withdraw at any time with full data deletion.

## 3. Assistive BAI — making it real

### 3.1 The right

- A citizen with profound communication disability has the right to BAI assistive technology on equivalent terms to a citizen with hearing aid or wheelchair access.
- Cost should not be a barrier; provision through CivicCare or CivicHealth depending on jurisdiction.
- Assistive BAI integration with the Civic Wallet allows full participation in civic life.

### 3.2 Engineering

- Assistive BAI runs on citizen-owned device with citizen-held keys.
- Local processing where possible; cloud only for hard cases under explicit consent.
- Standards-based interoperability across BAI vendors.
- No telemetry to state or commercial parties.

### 3.3 Privacy

- Neural data encrypted at rest with citizen key.
- Decryption only with citizen presence and consent.
- Cloud processing (where consented) uses sealed enclaves with verifiable code.
- Data retention minimized; deletion on request.

### 3.4 Recourse

- Citizens who experience harm from assistive BAI have full recourse: appeal, ombudsperson, courts.
- Vendors of certified BAI subject to liability.
- Sovereign Trust Officer monitors patterns of harm.

### 3.5 Inclusion

- Plain-language information about BAI options.
- Multiple BAI vendor choices.
- Independent advice on BAI selection (not vendor-driven).
- Family and carer support included where citizen consents.

## 4. Citizen ownership of neural data

### 4.1 The discipline

- Neural data is the most personal data humans produce.
- It belongs to the citizen, period.
- The state cannot subpoena neural data except under judicial process specific to a serious criminal matter, with technical assessor support, and with strict scope limits.
- Even then, the state cannot decrypt without the citizen's keys; it can only seek court order to compel.

### 4.2 Storage

- On citizen-held devices with hardware-backed keys.
- Cloud sync optional, end-to-end encrypted with citizen-held keys.
- Backups citizen-controlled.

### 4.3 Sharing

- Citizen-initiated only.
- Granular consent: which signals, which purposes, which durations.
- Revocable at any time.
- Sharing with researchers possible through Civic Data Trusts under strict purpose constraints and with explicit consent per project.

### 4.4 Deletion

- Citizen may delete at any time.
- Deletion is real (key destruction); not just hidden.
- Deletion confirmed.

### 4.5 Inheritance

- Neural data does not transfer to heirs by default.
- Citizen may specify in advance how neural data is handled at death.
- Default: deletion upon death registration.

## 5. Cognitive liberty

A right that becomes critical as BAI emerges.

### 5.1 The principle

- Citizens have the right to determine the contents and processes of their own minds.
- The state cannot intervene in cognition without explicit informed consent.
- Mental privacy is constitutional.

### 5.2 Operationalization

- No state interface that writes to neural systems without consent.
- No state monitoring of neural activity for civic purposes.
- No nudges, persuasion, or manipulation through neural channels.
- Anti-coercion safeguards: even consented access requires technical mechanisms preventing coercive use.

### 5.3 Forbidden

- BAI used to influence voting.
- BAI used to influence purchasing or commercial decisions in civic surfaces.
- BAI used in interrogation.
- BAI used to assess loyalty or compliance.
- BAI used to predict criminal behavior.

### 5.4 The cognitive liberty oath

CivicOS deployments require sovereigns to commit constitutionally or statutorily to cognitive liberty. Sovereigns failing this commitment are not eligible for BAI capability tier.

## 6. Medical use governance

Medical BAI (B-C, B-D, B-E) operates under medical regulation, not generic platform governance.

### 6.1 Medical authority

- Sovereign medical authority (typically health regulator) oversees medical BAI.
- Medical practitioner involvement required.
- Informed consent processes per medical norms.
- Outcome monitoring per medical norms.

### 6.2 Integration with CivicHealth

- BAI medical records part of longitudinal EHR with strict privacy regime.
- Citizen controls who sees neural-related health data.
- Aggregation only for legitimate public health purposes with consent.

### 6.3 Research

- Clinical research on BAI under standard medical research ethics.
- Civic Data Trust governance for research access.
- Citizen consent per study, not generic.
- Re-consent for new uses.

## 7. The forbidden list (BAI-specific)

CivicOS will not, under any circumstance, in any era:

- Use BAI for state surveillance.
- Permit commercial extraction of neural data within CivicOS jurisdictions.
- Permit BAI for authentication in civic surfaces.
- Permit BAI for persuasion in civic surfaces.
- Permit coercive BAI intervention.
- Permit BAI use in elections.
- Permit BAI for civic decisions about individuals.
- Permit cross-border transfer of citizen neural data.
- Permit foreign vendor access to citizen neural data.
- Permit BAI use in criminal interrogation absent strictest judicial oversight (and even there, jurisdictions may forbid absolutely).
- Permit BAI in workplaces operated by the state for performance monitoring.
- Permit BAI for predictive classification of citizens.
- Permit BAI as a condition for accessing services.
- Permit BAI use without citizen-held keys.

This list grows; it does not shrink.

Sovereigns deploying BAI are recommended to constitutionalize:

- Right to mental privacy.
- Right to cognitive liberty.
- Right to deletion of neural data.
- Right to assistive BAI for severe disability (positive right).
- Prohibition of state surveillance through BAI (negative right).
- Prohibition of commercial extraction of neural data.

Without these constitutional anchors, BAI deployment risks exceed acceptable thresholds.

## 9. Specific scenarios

### 9.1 A locked-in citizen communicates through BAI

- Citizen consents at medical assessment.
- Vendor-neutral BAI provided through CivicCare.
- Neural data encrypted with citizen-held keys.
- Civic Wallet integration allows full identity, payments, voting, civic participation.
- Citizen retains all rights and dignity.

### 9.2 A research lab requests aggregate neural data

- Research project reviewed by Civic Data Trust ethics board.
- Affected citizens individually consent per project.
- Aggregation through differential privacy.
- No re-identification possible.
- Researcher access through secure enclave.
- Findings published; citizens informed of aggregate results.

### 9.3 An employer wants to monitor cognitive load

- Forbidden in state employment.
- Heavily regulated in private employment per labor law.
- CivicOS does not provide tooling for this.
- Workers have rights to refuse without retaliation.

### 9.4 A criminal investigation seeks neural evidence

- Subject to strictest judicial process.
- Technical assessor support required.
- Many jurisdictions forbid absolutely.
- Where permitted: subject's keys still required; consent or compelled access via court order.
- Subject of investigation has counsel and full procedural rights.

### 9.5 A foreign government demands neural data

- Refused absolutely.
- Cross-border transfer of citizen neural data is forbidden.
- Even MLATs do not extend to neural data without specific local statute.
- Sovereign Trust Officer immediately notified of any such demand.

## 16. The BAI north star

Brain-adjacent interfaces are humanity's most personal information frontier. They will arrive. The question is whether they arrive as transformative assistive technology that includes the previously excluded, or as surveillance and manipulation infrastructure that erodes cognitive liberty itself.

CivicOS's commitment is unambiguous: the assistive use is supported, the broader use is constrained, the surveillance use is forbidden absolutely. Citizens own their neural data. The state does not surveil through BAI. Commercial extraction is illegal in CivicOS jurisdictions. Cognitive liberty is constitutional.

This is not technological caution; it is constitutional clarity. The discipline grows with capability. The forbidden list grows with capability. The sovereignty hardens with capability.

When BAI becomes a tool of state surveillance or commercial extraction in any CivicOS jurisdiction, the platform has failed and that jurisdiction must reform — or risk loss of standing in the planetary CivicOS community.

The most personal data we produce remains ours. The platform exists to ensure that. Anything else is the deepest betrayal a state could commit.

The discipline is constitutional. The technology is citizen-owned. The use is consented. The future is one we keep our minds for ourselves.
