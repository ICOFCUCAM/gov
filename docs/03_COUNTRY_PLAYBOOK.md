# CivicOS — Country Adoption Playbook (Companion 03)

A repeatable, opinionated playbook for taking a country from kickoff to full DPI in 36 months, with phased value delivery every 6 months. Adaptable to micro-states (Pacific, Caribbean), mid-size African countries, and large multi-region states.

---

## 1. Pre-engagement

### 1.1 Qualification questions

Before signing, get crisp answers to:

1. **Sponsorship.** Who at head-of-state / cabinet level will own this for 5 years?
2. **Legal authority.** Is there a digital-government law enabling cross-agency data exchange? If not, what's the path?
3. **Identity baseline.** Existing civil registration coverage? Biometric system? National ID law?
4. **Payments baseline.** RTGS? Instant payments? Mobile money penetration? CBDC ambition?
5. **Connectivity.** Broadband coverage? Mobile coverage? Power reliability?
6. **Talent.** ICT graduates per year? Local integrator capacity?
7. **Funding.** Multi-year domestic budget commitment + multilateral co-financing?
8. **Sovereignty stance.** Sovereign cloud preferred? Hyperscaler tolerated? Air-gap requirements?
9. **Existing systems.** Oracle EBS? SAP? Bespoke mainframes? Migration appetite?
10. **Political timeline.** Election cycles within next 36 months? Risk of policy reversal?

If 6+ are red, recommend a foundational engagement only (PKI + identity + wallet) and revisit.

### 1.2 Engagement structure

- Sovereign Steering Committee (Cabinet-level): quarterly.
- Program Management Office: weekly.
- Technical Working Groups (one per module): bi-weekly.
- Citizen Council (civil society + media + academia): quarterly.
- Independent Audit Office: standing access.

## 2. Phase 0 — Foundations (months 0–6)

Goal: Build the machinery that lets every later phase be fast.

| Workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| Legal | Digital-government law passed (or amended); data protection law in force; AI authority established |
| Sovereign cloud | Region 1 live; HSM cluster commissioned; PKI ceremony complete |
| Talent | 50 government engineers trained on CivicOS kernel; 5 integrators certified |
| Identity | CivicID enrollment infrastructure live in 3 pilot districts |
| Wallet | Civic Wallet beta in app stores; USSD twin live |
| DataExchange | CivicBus central registry live; 3 first-mover ministries onboarded |
| Trust | National PKI operational; trust services authority designated |
| Payments | Central bank integration spec signed; sandbox live |
| Governance | Sovereign Steering Committee active; Citizen Council seated |

Exit criteria: a citizen can enroll, receive a CivicID credential in their wallet, and authenticate to a sample service.

## 3. Phase 1 — DPI live (months 6–18)

Goal: Make the rails real.

| Workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| Identity | Coverage expanded to 30%; mobile enrollment teams in all provinces |
| Wallet | 1M+ active wallets |
| Payments | CivicPay live with all systemic banks; mobile money operators integrated; first programmable disbursement (e.g., emergency grant) executed |
| Tax | Tax authority on CivicBus; e-invoicing in shadow mode for large corporates |
| Welfare | Social registry consolidated; pilot CCT delivered through wallet |
| Civil Registration | Births and deaths digital end-to-end |
| Procurement | OCDS publication live; vendor registry on platform |
| Comms | CivicVoice operational; emergency alert tested |
| Analytics | National dashboard for cabinet, populated with first 10 KPIs |

Exit criteria: citizen can pay a government fee from their wallet; a ministry can disburse a benefit to 10,000 citizens with one click.

## 4. Phase 2 — First modules (months 12–30)

Goal: Replace 80% of high-friction citizen interactions.

Suggested module sequence (prioritizes citizen value and revenue payback):

1. Tax e-filing (revenue payback < 18 months).
2. Welfare disbursement (visible quick win, multilateral co-funding likely).
3. Driver and vehicle (visible to a wide population).
4. Civil registration full (births, deaths, marriages, divorce).
5. Two pioneer municipalities on CivicCity.
6. Permits (business registration, building permits).
7. Health module pilot in 1 province.
8. Education module pilot in 1 province.

Exit criteria: 50% of in-scope citizen interactions are digital; CSI (Citizen Satisfaction Index) measurable.

---

## 5. Phase 3 — Expansion (months 24–48)

Goal: Full ministry coverage; AI ubiquity.

- All ministries on platform.
- Marketplace v1 with 20+ certified apps.
- AI assistants in 5 services.
- Predictive governance integrated into budget cycle.
- Cross-border interoperability with 1 neighbor (identity recognition or payments).

---

## 6. Phase 4 — Sovereign capability transfer (months 36–60)

Goal: Country owns its destiny.

- National operating company (often public-private with state golden share) takes over operations.
- CivicOS Inc. provides upstream platform updates and L3 support.
- 200+ certified national engineers.
- Country becomes a regional reference; hosts cross-country exchange visits.

---

## 7. Talent strategy

- **Civic Academy** in-country: 6-month bootcamps producing platform engineers.
- **University curricula** updated for DPI engineering.
- **Apprenticeship** program inside ministries.
- **Senior secondments** from CivicOS Inc. and partner integrators, with mandated knowledge transfer KPIs.
- **Diaspora reintegration** programs.

---

## 8. Change management

- Communications campaign per module launch (citizen-facing + officer-facing).
- Officer training certified per module before activation.
- Champion network in each ministry.
- Resistance management: identify legacy stakeholders early; offer dignified transitions.

## 9. Funding mix

| Source | Typical share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic budget | 40–60% | Recurrent costs, salaries |
| Multilateral grants/loans | 30–50% | World Bank ID4D, AfDB, EU, GIZ, JICA |
| Bilateral cooperation | 5–15% | Estonia, Singapore, India, etc. |
| User fees / shared savings | 5–10% | Self-funding once revenue modules live |

Insist on transparent procurement and open-book accounting from CivicOS Inc. to maintain political legitimacy.

---

## 10. Common failure modes (and how to avoid them)

1. **Big-bang migrations** → Phase strictly; never migrate everything at once.
2. **Vendor capture** → Open standards, escrow, exit clauses, multi-vendor for critical components.
3. **Politically captured procurement** → Transparency, oversight, OCDS.
4. **Capacity collapse after consultants leave** → Talent strategy non-negotiable.
5. **Connectivity assumed** → Offline-first design always.
6. **Ignored civil society** → Citizen Council with teeth.
7. **Underestimated identity issues** → Do not skip the no-biometrics path or appeal mechanisms.
8. **Surveillance creep** → Hard guardrails; independent oversight; public capability registry.
9. **AI deployed before evals** → Decision class governance enforced from day one.
10. **Election-cycle disruption** → Institutional design, not personality-based; constitutional anchoring of platform mandate.

## 12. Exit and replaceability commitments

CivicOS Inc. publishes and maintains:

- A documented data export format per module.
- A "platform exit" runbook — actually executed in a sandbox annually.
- An open-source kernel keeping the country operational even if the company disappears.
- Source escrow with the sovereign.
- A no-coercion charter; refusal of foreign government data demands without local court order.

These commitments are part of the contract, not marketing.
