# CivicOS — Physical Infrastructure Loss and Recovery (Companion 134)

This companion specifies how CivicOS handles loss of physical infrastructure — data centers destroyed by disaster, communications cables severed, power systems failed long-term, hardware lost in conflict — and recovery from such loss. It complements Companion 05 (SLO and DR catalog), Companion 11 §3 (infrastructure evolution), Companion 30 (crisis operations), Companion 109 (operational failure modes), and Companion 122 (first hours after catastrophe) by being specifically about physical infrastructure recovery.

The thesis: **physical infrastructure can be destroyed; CivicOS must continue serving citizens through and after such loss**. Backup data centers, redundant communications, restorable hardware supplies, sovereign capability rebuilding — all matter. Recovery requires pre-positioned redundancy and disciplined rebuilding.

The discipline: multi-region active-active for tier-0; backup data centers offsite; redundant communications including satellite; restorable hardware supplies; sovereign capacity for rebuilding; cross-sovereign mutual aid where applicable; civil society and citizen engagement on recovery; honest about loss; long-term recovery commitment.

## 3. Pre-positioned redundancy

Per Companion 05 §4:

### 3.1 Mechanisms

- Multi-region active-active for tier-0 services.
- Geographic diversity in datacenter siting.
- Multi-cable, multi-route communications.
- Multi-orbit satellite backup.
- Backup power infrastructure.
- HSM geographic distribution.

### 3.2 Discipline

- Anti-single-point-of-failure.
- Anti-shared-fate (datacenters not in same earthquake zone, flood plain, conflict zone).
- Honest assessment of redundancy.

### 3.3 Forbidden

- Sovereign-critical infrastructure with single physical point of failure.
- Backup infrastructure that shares fate with primary.
- Hidden redundancy gaps.

## 17. The physical infrastructure loss and recovery north star

Physical infrastructure can be destroyed; CivicOS must continue serving citizens through and after such loss. CivicOS supports recovery through multi-region active-active for tier-0, geographic diversity, redundant communications, hardware supply diversity, sovereign rebuilding capacity, cross-sovereign mutual aid, civil society engagement, honest acknowledgment, and long-term commitment.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of recovery favoritism, civilian infrastructure targeting, hidden loss, or political instrumentalization of recovery — it has failed at continuity service to citizens. Capability without recovery discipline is not progress; it is the institutionalization of platform fragility.

When the platform supports physical infrastructure loss and recovery with discipline — redundancy, civilian protection, mutual aid, civil society engagement, and long-term commitment — it earns the right to be infrastructure for civilizations that face disasters, conflicts, and uncertainty across decades.

The discipline is daily. The redundancy is multi-region. The civilian protection is structural. The mutual aid is real. The civil society engagement is honored. The honest acknowledgment is constant.

Physical infrastructure is vulnerable. The platform's response to vulnerability shapes whether citizens experience disaster as platform-mediated continuity or platform-mediated additional harm. Anything less abandons citizens during their hardest moments to additional harm from platform failure.
