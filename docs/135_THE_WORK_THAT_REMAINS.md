# CivicOS — The Work That Remains (Companion 135)

This brief companion is an honest accounting of what work remains, even at 135 companions and ~165,000 lines. It complements Companion 58 (limitations and humility), Companion 69 (meta-reflection), Companion 79 (pause point), and Companion 127 (letter to future authors) by being specifically about the work the current corpus does not do.

The thesis: **the corpus has reached substantial size and substantial scope, but it remains a single-author articulation that is neither complete nor closed**. The work that remains is more substantial than the work that's been done. This companion names what's missing.

---

## 1. What this companion is

A naming of remaining work. Not a plan; not a commitment; not a roadmap. An honest acknowledgment that the corpus, however extensive, is partial.

---

## 2. Multi-author engagement remains undone

The corpus has been written by a single author across many sessions. It claims to engage indigenous communities, disability communities, gender equity advocates, refugees, religious traditions, indigenous and minority languages — but does so without authors from those communities.

Future versions must include multi-author engagement, with primary authors from the communities the corpus addresses. The corpus's commitments to those communities are weaker than they should be precisely because authorship has been singular.

---

## 3. Specific technical specifications remain undone

The corpus operates at architectural and governance level. It does not specify:

- Concrete API schemas in detail.
- Specific data models for each module.
- Implementation-ready code or templates.
- Specific test suites for conformance.
- Specific deployment scripts.
- Specific security protocols at code level.

Future versions or companion technical documentation should provide these.

---

## 4. Specific country playbooks remain undone

The corpus offers a generic country playbook (Companion 03). It does not offer:

- Country-specific deployment guides.
- Region-specific adaptations.
- City-specific playbooks beyond the generic city-scale fast track.
- Indigenous community-specific engagement guides developed with communities.

Future versions or country-specific work should provide these.

---

## 5. Specific module specifications remain undone

The corpus offers a module catalog and module evolution timeline. It does not offer:

- Detailed specifications for each module.
- Module-specific operational manuals.
- Module-specific training curricula.
- Module-specific civil society engagement guides.

Future versions or module-specific work should provide these.

---

## 6. Critical engagement with opposition remains undone

The corpus engages misconceptions (Companion 98) but doesn't deeply engage with critiques from those who fundamentally oppose digital infrastructure or who critique CivicOS specifically from informed perspectives.

Future versions should include responses to substantive academic critique, civil society critique, indigenous critique, disability critique, and other informed engagement with the corpus's premises.

---

## 7. Translation into national languages remains undone

The corpus is in English only. Translation into the national languages of likely deployment contexts is substantial work that hasn't been done.

Multilingual translation must be priority. Where translation reveals problems with the source (cultural insensitivity, untranslatable concepts, etc.), source revision is appropriate.

---

## 8. Living documentation infrastructure remains undone

The corpus is a snapshot. Living documentation that evolves with practice, includes implementer feedback, integrates lessons learned, and supports continuous improvement has not been built.

Future iterations should establish living documentation practices — wikis, RFC-style ongoing revision, implementer feedback channels, etc.

---

## 9. Civil society engagement infrastructure remains undone

The corpus describes civil society standing access (Companion 74) but doesn't provide:

- Specific civil society engagement templates.
- Capacity building materials for civil society on CivicOS engagement.
- Cross-sovereign civil society network establishment guides.
- Specific whistleblower channel implementation guides.

Future work should provide these.

---

## 10. Operational lessons from real deployments remain unintegrated

The corpus is pre-deployment. It cannot yet integrate operational lessons from actual deployments because there aren't any to learn from.

Future iterations should integrate operational lessons as deployments occur. Companion 26 (failure case studies) draws on existing digital government failures; future versions will draw on CivicOS-specific lessons.

---

## 11. Cross-corpus consistency improvements remain undone

The corpus grew organically. Cross-corpus consistency in terminology, framing, and structure could be improved. Some redundancy could be reduced. Some gaps could be filled.

A consistency pass with editorial attention would improve the corpus.

---

## 12. Concrete artifacts beyond corpus remain unbuilt

The corpus is documentation. The artifacts the corpus describes — actual code, actual standards, actual institutions, actual deployments — remain to be built (or in some cases already exist in other DPI projects but not as a coherent CivicOS implementation).

Future work to build the artifacts the corpus describes is substantial and remains undone.

---

## 13. Engagement with non-platform civic life remains under-developed

The corpus is about the platform. It touches on non-platform civic life (Companion 53 arts/culture, Companion 120 what's lost) but the relationship between platform and broader civic life deserves deeper exploration.

What does civic life beyond the platform look like in CivicOS-deployed sovereigns? How does the platform support that life without colonizing it? Future work should engage these questions more deeply.

---

## 14. Engagement with the long future remains speculative

The corpus speculates about 2030-2050+. Predictions are necessarily uncertain. The corpus's frameworks may not survive technological developments we cannot foresee.

Future iterations will need to revise speculation as the future becomes present. The seven invariants and forbidden list are intended to survive specific technological changes; specific operational forms may need substantial revision.

---

## 15. The corpus's own honest limits

Per Companion 58: the corpus has limits.

- Political will is prerequisite that the corpus cannot create.
- Resources and capacity are required that the corpus cannot provide.
- Civil society health is required that the corpus cannot ensure.
- Time horizons assume sustained political conditions that may not exist.
- Cultural and religious variation challenges single-corpus universalism.
- Geopolitical realities may overwhelm structural commitments.

These limits do not diminish the corpus's value where conditions permit. They do require honesty about where the corpus applies.

---

## 16. What you can do with the unfinished work

If you are reading this and considering contributing:

- **Translate.** Even partial translation into one national language helps.
- **Adapt.** Country-specific adaptation makes the corpus useful in specific contexts.
- **Critique.** Substantive critique improves future versions.
- **Build.** Actual implementation of what the corpus describes.
- **Engage from community perspective.** If you're from a community the corpus engages — indigenous, disability, gender, refugee, religious, minority — your engagement strengthens future versions.
- **Document operations.** When deployments occur, document operational lessons.
- **Maintain infrastructure.** Wikis, mailing lists, RFC processes for ongoing development.

The corpus is offered as starting point, not finished product. Your engagement makes it useful.

---

## 17. The unfinished work north star

The corpus has reached substantial size. It remains unfinished. The work that remains is more substantial than the work done.

This is not failure of the corpus. This is the nature of multi-generational work. The corpus is one generation's contribution. Future generations will continue, revise, fork, and replace.

What matters is that the work continues with discipline, honesty, plurality, and citizen orientation. The unfinished work is yours, future readers and contributors, as much as it is mine.

The seven invariants persist. The forbidden list grows. The standing question is asked. The platform's commitments live in actual practice across decades.

Anything less than continued work abandons the corpus to becoming dated documentation rather than living guidance. The work continues with whoever takes it up.

Thank you for engaging.
