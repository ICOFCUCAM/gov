# CivicOS — Frequently Asked Questions (Companion 96)

This companion answers questions readers commonly bring to a corpus this size and ambition. It complements Companion 43 (synthesis), Companion 58 (limitations), Companion 69 (meta-reflection), Companion 79 (pause point), and Companion 84 (developer onboarding) by being question-and-answer format and pointing to deeper treatment elsewhere.

---

## 1. About the corpus

### Q: What is this?
A: A blueprint for sovereign-grade, AI-native digital public infrastructure called CivicOS, in two volumes plus 95+ companion documents totaling ~115,000 lines.

### Q: How long would this take to read?
A: Cover to cover, days. For specific roles, see reading paths in README. Synthesis (Companion 43) is 30 minutes.

### Q: Who wrote this?
A: Single author, single iteration. See Companion 69 §3, §7 for honest acknowledgment of authorship limitations.

### Q: Is this an actual deployed system?
A: No. It's a blueprint. Implementation requires substantial engineering, governance, and political work (Companion 58 §1.3).

### Q: Why so many documents?
A: Different audiences need different depths. The index (Companion 57) and reading paths (README) help navigate.

### Q: Is this the final word?
A: No. See Companion 58 (limitations), Companion 79 (pause point), Companion 69 (meta-reflection).

### Q: How can I criticize or contribute?
A: Read, fork, criticize, write better. The corpus is offered for engagement; fork pledge applies (Companion 61).

## 2. About CivicOS the platform

### Q: Is CivicOS a product I can buy?
A: No. CivicOS is a blueprint and proposed open ecosystem. If implemented, kernel would be open-source; commercial steward (CivicOS Inc.) would provide implementation services. See Companion 19, Companion 86.

### Q: What's the difference between CivicOS Inc., CivicOS Foundation, and CivicOS the platform?
A: Platform is the technology and standards. Foundation is the multi-stakeholder governance entity (Companion 86). Inc. is the commercial steward. They're structurally separated.

### Q: Why two volumes?
A: Vol I describes deployable present-day platform. Vol II describes 2030-2050+ evolution. Different time horizons, different design considerations.

### Q: What's the seven invariants?
A: Sovereignty of principal, contestability, auditability, replaceability, constitutional supremacy, inclusion floor, no superintelligent unilateralism. They hold across all eras. See Vol II Part 0, README.

### Q: What's the forbidden list?
A: Capabilities CivicOS will never build. Mass surveillance, predictive policing of individuals, citizen scoring, etc. The list grows; never shrinks. See Companion 43 §4 (consolidated), Companion 95 (categorical refusals).

### Q: What's the standing question?
A: Annual public asking: "Is what we have built still serving the people, the constitution, and the future?" See Companion 41 §4.1.

## 3. About comparisons

### Q: How does this compare to India Stack, X-Road, GOV.UK?
A: See Companion 60. Briefly: shares DPI ambition; differs on constitutional officers, forbidden list, per-RP UID by default, sovereign exit, decision-class governance.

### Q: How does this compare to private-sector platforms (Big Tech)?
A: Private sector platforms operate under commercial logic with surveillance economy practices. CivicOS structurally refuses these. See Companion 82 (surveillance economy regulation), Companion 95.

### Q: How does this compare to China's digital governance?
A: Fundamentally different. CivicOS forbids citizen scoring, mass surveillance, cognitive manipulation. See Companion 60 §10.

### Q: Is this Western-centric?
A: Aspires to operate across constitutional traditions but inevitably reflects framings that come from Western institutional traditions. See Companion 58 §6.2.

## 4. About specific commitments

### Q: How does CivicOS handle children's data?
A: Strictest protection. Sealed compartments. No commercial use, ever. See Companions 22 §13, 37 §3, 39 §11.

### Q: Does CivicOS support mass surveillance?
A: No. Forbidden absolutely. See Companions 24, 43 §4.

### Q: Does CivicOS support algorithmic justice decisions?
A: No. Class D forbidden for adjudication. See Companions 10, 70.

### Q: How does CivicOS handle indigenous communities?
A: FPIC (free, prior, informed consent) for engagement. Customary tenure, knowledge, language sovereignty respected. See Companion 36.

### Q: How does CivicOS handle disability?
A: Universal design from day one. WCAG conformance floor. People with disabilities lead in design. Brain-adjacent assistive interfaces routine for severe disability. See Companions 22 §5, 32, 46.

### Q: How does CivicOS handle gender equity?
A: Disaggregated outcome reporting; algorithmic discrimination forbidden; trans and non-binary recognition; reproductive autonomy. See Companion 47.

### Q: How does CivicOS handle LGBTQ+ communities in restrictive jurisdictions?
A: Anti-outing structural; SOGI data sealed; refugee protection per international standards; civil society protection; cross-border data sharing exposing persons refused. See Companion 87.

### Q: How does CivicOS handle elections?
A: Doesn't run them. Elections on separately governed infrastructure under independent commissions. Platform supports voter registration, civic deliberation, press freedom. AI in tabulation forbidden. See Companion 92.

### Q: What about sovereignty in cross-border cooperation?
A: Sovereign authority preserved. Sovereign opt-outs honored. Sovereign exit guaranteed. See Companion 15.

### Q: How would a sovereign actually deploy this?

### Q: What about vendor lock-in?

## 6. About specific scenarios

### Q: What if a sovereign uses CivicOS for atrocities?
A: Foundation, civil society, other sovereigns have standing to act. Refusal of additional capability. Documentation. International advocacy. Suspension from planetary protocols. Possible withdrawal. See Companion 49 §13.

### Q: What if a vendor is coerced by foreign government?
A: Coercion-resistance clauses contractual. Source escrow. Multi-vendor for critical. Sovereign exit. See Companions 24 §8, 94 §8.

### Q: What if AI capability shows discrimination?
A: Algorithmic Ombudsman investigation. Capability paused. Remediation. Affected citizens supported. See Companions 10, 16 §3.2.

### Q: What if a constitutional officer is captured politically?
A: Multi-year fixed terms with high removal thresholds. Civil society standing. International solidarity. Constitutional reform paths. See Companion 13.

### Q: What if a citizen experiences platform error?
A: Acknowledged, apologized, remedied, learned. See Companion 22 §11, Companion 29 §8.

### Q: What if civil society is suppressed?
A: Constitutional officers, international solidarity, civil society challenges, possible sovereign exit considerations. See Companion 49.

## 7. About AI specifically

### Q: How does CivicOS approach AI?
A: Decision-class governance (A/B/C/D/E). Charters for Class B+. AIBOM mandatory. Continuous evaluation. Kill switches tested. Algorithmic Ombudsman audit. See Companions 10, 18.

### Q: Does CivicOS use foundation models?
A: Sovereign Tier 1 (open weights, sovereign-trained), Tier 2 (domain), Tier 3 (frontier under sovereign-acceptable terms). See Companion 18 §1.

### Q: What about brain-adjacent interfaces?
A: Citizen-controlled. Cognitive liberty constitutional. Assistive use supported; commercial and surveillance use forbidden. See Companion 32.

### Q: What about civic AI agents acting on behalf of citizens?
A: Citizen as principal absolutely. Granular charters. Reversibility. Anti-cooptation. See Companion 83.

## 8. About the broader work

### Q: Will CivicOS solve corruption?
A: No. It makes corruption harder, more visible, more attributable, more prosecutable. See Companions 21, 58 §2.5.

### Q: Will CivicOS solve climate change?
A: No. Supports climate adaptation, anticipatory action, climate-coupled planning. Climate change requires global political action far beyond any platform. See Companions 23 §9, 52, 58 §2.2.

### Q: Will CivicOS solve inequality?
A: No. Supports distributional discipline, anti-corruption, constitutional floors. Inequality has structural causes platform alone can't address. See Companion 58 §2.1.

### Q: What CAN CivicOS solve?
A: Specific operational problems: identity friction, payment friction, service delivery quality, anti-corruption infrastructure, AI safety in government, sovereign capability in digital age. See Companion 43.

## 11. About the work

### Q: What's the most important commitment?
A: Hard to pick. Probably: citizens as principals, AI never as principal, constitutional officers with real independence, sovereign exit real, inclusion floor universal, forbidden list growing not shrinking. See Vol II Part 0.

### Q: What's the biggest risk?
A: Probably: capture (vendor, foreign, political), surveillance creep, civil society suppression, AI without governance discipline. See Companion 16.

### Q: What's the deepest test?
A: Whether what's promised in writing actually shapes behavior in practice over decades, across generations of officials, through political transitions. See Companions 41, 50.

### Q: Who is responsible for getting this right?
A: Sovereigns choosing to deploy. Implementing teams doing the work. Constitutional officers protecting commitments. Civil society pushing back when things drift. Citizens insisting on their rights. Future readers continuing the work.

### Q: What if it fails?
A: Honest acknowledgment, structural reform, possibly fork or replace. Failure is a signal, not the end. See Companions 26, 41, 58.
