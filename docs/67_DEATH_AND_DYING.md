# CivicOS — Death, Dying, and Bereavement Infrastructure (Companion 67)

This companion specifies how CivicOS serves people in death, dying, and bereavement. It complements Companion 22 §14 (end-of-life dignity in error), Companion 38 §13 (clinical end-of-life), Companion 51 §14 (religious practice), and Companion 37 §8 (intergenerational) by going deep on the operational and ethical design of a domain often neglected in digital governance.

The thesis: **how a society treats its dying and bereaved reveals its character**. Death is the universal experience that connects every citizen to every other across all eras. Digital systems that handle death badly cause real harm: continued billing after death, repeated demands for proof of loss, exclusion from bereavement support, breach of dignity in the most vulnerable moments. CivicOS is built to handle death and bereavement with structural compassion, cultural and religious accommodation, anti-bureaucracy, and reparative posture toward family error.

The discipline: death registration triggers automatic suspension of obligations; estate processes streamlined; bereaved family supported without burden; cultural and religious end-of-life respected; advance directives accessible and honored; inheritance per applicable law and custom; memorial and commemoration on community terms; intergenerational continuity preserved.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Death is universal.** Every citizen will face it; how the platform handles it matters.
2. **Bereaved family supported, not burdened.** Death triggers compassion, not paperwork.
3. **Cultural and religious accommodation.** Diverse death and mourning practices respected.
4. **Advance directives honored.** Citizen autonomy in end-of-life.
5. **Anti-commercialization of bereavement.** Mourning is not market.
6. **Intergenerational continuity.** Inheritance, memory, lineage.
7. **Memorial and commemoration on community terms.**
8. **Anti-fraud with proportionate burden.** Not on grieving.
9. **Privacy preserved through and after death.**
10. **Death of state interactions is honest acknowledgment.**

## 2. Death registration

### 2.1 Mechanisms

- Hospital death triggers registration through CivicHealth.
- Home death registration through community health workers or registrars.
- Cross-jurisdictional death (citizens dying abroad) through CivicForeign.
- Death certificate issuance promptly.
- Multilingual.
- Multiple original copies as needed.

### 2.2 Triggered actions

Upon death registration:

- Wallet deactivated (with safe period for family discovery of contents).
- CivicID status updated.
- Pension transitions automated.
- Tax obligations cease (estate taxation per applicable law).
- Voter roll updated.
- Beneficiary notifications.
- Insurance claims initiated.
- Employer notifications.
- Service subscriptions paused.

### 2.3 Discipline

- Anti-fraud with proportionate burden (not on bereaved family).
- Verification through hospital/health worker authority.
- Anti-corruption in registration.

### 2.4 Forbidden

- Continued billing after death.
- Repeated demand for proof of death.
- Discriminatory delays in death registration.

- Probate process through CivicJustice.
- Estate administration support.
- Asset transfer through CivicLand, CivicPay, etc.
- Beneficiary notification.
- Tax handling (estate per applicable law).

### 4.2 Discipline

- Streamlined for routine cases.
- Cultural and religious inheritance practices respected within constitutional limits.
- Anti-fraud with proportionate burden.
- Independent oversight in disputed cases.

### 4.3 Forbidden

- Discriminatory inheritance practices outside constitutional bounds.
- Algorithmic estate decisions absent human review.
- Commercial exploitation through estate processes.

## 5. Inheritance per culture and law

### 5.1 The principle

Inheritance practices vary widely across cultures and religions. CivicOS accommodates within constitutional rights frameworks.

### 5.2 Mechanisms

- Customary inheritance recognized where lawful.
- Religious inheritance recognized where lawful.
- Joint and family ownership patterns supported.
- Indigenous inheritance per community practice (Companion 36).
- Constitutional rights (women's inheritance, etc.) prevail in conflicts.

### 5.3 Discipline

- Vulnerable individuals (women, children, dependents) protected.
- Bilateral evolution between state and community.
- Cultural sensitivity without dilution of rights.

### 5.4 Forbidden

- Discriminatory inheritance practice violating constitutional rights.
- Exclusion of women from inheritance contrary to law.
- Algorithmic inheritance decisions absent human judgment.

## 12. Right to be forgotten after death

### 12.1 The principle

Some communities want digital traces of deceased removed; some want them preserved. CivicOS accommodates per cultural and family wishes within statutory limits.

### 12.2 Mechanisms

- Family request for removal (within statutory limits — some records must persist for historical and legal purposes).
- Cryptographic deletion via key destruction where applicable.
- Memorial preservation where chosen.

### 12.3 Discipline

- Family wishes respected.
- Statutory archival requirements honored.
- Cultural variation accommodated.

### 12.4 Forbidden

- Commercial preservation against family wishes.
- State preservation absent legitimate purpose.
- Discrimination in deletion processes.

### 13.2 Mechanisms

- Memorial where families and communities seek.
- Anti-recurrence measures.

### 13.3 Discipline

- Cultural and religious accommodation.
- Long-term commitment.

### 13.4 Forbidden

- Cover-up of institutional death.
- Suppression of survivor accounts.
- Politicization of memorial.
- Discrimination in reparative measures.

## 16. The death and bereavement north star

How a society treats its dying and bereaved reveals its character. CivicOS is built so that this most universal experience is met with structural compassion, cultural and religious accommodation, anti-bureaucracy, and reparative posture for institutional failure.

Death triggers automatic suspension of obligations. Estate processes streamlined. Bereaved family supported without burden. Cultural and religious end-of-life respected. Advance directives honored. Inheritance per applicable law and custom. Memorial on community terms. Privacy through and after death.

When CivicOS becomes a tool that bills the dead, demands repeated proof of loss, excludes the bereaved, exploits grief commercially, or covers up institutional death — it has failed citizens at their most vulnerable. Capability without compassion in death is not progress; it is the institutionalization of cruelty in mourning.

When the platform serves people in death and bereavement with dignity, accommodation, support, and honesty — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies that honor their members across the full arc of life and beyond.

The discipline is daily. The compassion is structural. The cultural respect is non-negotiable. The dignity is preserved through and after death.

Death is universal. The platform's response to it is the deepest test of its commitments to dignity. Anything less abandons people in their most vulnerable moments.
