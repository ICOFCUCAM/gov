# CivicOS — Suicide Prevention and Mental Health Crisis Response (Companion 88)

This companion specifies how CivicOS supports suicide prevention and mental health crisis response. It complements Companion 38 §9 (mental health), Companion 22 §5.4 (vulnerability), Companion 30 (crisis operations), Companion 39 §14.2 (school-based mental health), Companion 37 §3 (children's protection), and Companion 87 (LGBTQ+) by going deep on a domain where digital infrastructure can genuinely save lives or — done badly — cause harm.

The thesis: **suicide is a leading cause of death across many populations and contexts; mental health crises affect millions; how digital infrastructure handles these moments determines outcomes for citizens at their most vulnerable**. CivicOS supports suicide prevention and mental health crisis response through accessible crisis lines, mental health professional dispatch (not police-default), trauma-informed service design, anti-stigma in service interactions, sealed compartment data protection, and structural support for those experiencing or surviving mental health crises.

The discipline: crisis lines accessible 24/7 across modalities; mental health professional response not police-default; trauma-informed throughout; data sealed; anti-stigma in design; survivor and lived-experience voices central; vulnerable populations supported; cross-sector coordination; international cooperation under sovereignty.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Crisis access universal.** 24/7 across modalities.
2. **Mental health professional response.** Not police-default for mental health crises.
3. **Trauma-informed throughout.** All interactions.
4. **Anti-stigma structural.** In design, language, service delivery.
5. **Data sealed.** Strictest privacy regime.
6. **Lived experience voices central.** In service design and improvement.
7. **Vulnerable populations supported.** Children, LGBTQ+, indigenous, post-conflict, etc.
8. **Cross-sector coordination.** Health, education, justice, social services.
9. **Means restriction supported.** Where evidence-based and ethical.
10. **Postvention for survivors.** Long-term support after suicide loss.

---

## 2. Crisis line infrastructure

### 2.1 Mechanisms

- 24/7 crisis lines accessible by phone, SMS, chat, and through Civic Wallet.
- Trained crisis counselors.
- Multilingual coverage.
- Anti-discrimination in service.
- Privacy strict (sealed compartment).
- Connection to ongoing support without coercion.

### 2.2 Discipline

- Anti-rote-response.
- Lived experience inclusion in counselor training.
- Cultural and contextual sensitivity.
- Anti-coercion (e.g., not threatening hospitalization to prevent disclosure).

### 2.3 Forbidden

- Use of crisis line data for adverse decisions (employment, custody, etc.).
- Surveillance through crisis line use.
- Discrimination in service.
- Coerced hospitalization absent narrow legitimate criteria.

---

## 3. Crisis response — not police-default

### 3.1 The principle

Mental health crises are health emergencies, not criminal events. Mental health professionals respond, not police as default.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- Mobile crisis teams (mental health professionals, peer supporters) dispatched for mental health crises.
- Co-response models (mental health + police where genuinely needed) not as default.
- Diversion from criminal justice for mental health crisis.
- Crisis stabilization centers as alternative to emergency rooms.
- Anti-criminalization of mental health crisis.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Dispatch protocols clear.
- Anti-discrimination in response.
- Trauma-informed interactions.
- Anti-violence in response.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Police-default for mental health crisis (where alternatives exist).
- Use of force in mental health crisis absent narrow legitimate need.
- Criminalization of mental health crisis.
- Algorithmic risk-scoring for mental health crisis response.

## 5. Data discipline

### 5.1 The principle

Mental health and crisis data is among the most sensitive personal data. Unauthorized disclosure can have severe consequences (employment, custody, immigration, etc.).

### 5.2 Mechanisms

- Sealed compartment storage (Companion 23 §3.2).
- Per-RP UID prevents cross-service correlation.
- Access strictly limited to clinical and crisis purposes.
- No mandatory reporting that deters help-seeking (with narrow exceptions for imminent harm).
- Survivor consent for any sharing.

### 5.3 Discipline

- Anti-cross-purpose access.
- Anti-aggregation.
- Anti-commercial-use.
- Anti-cross-border-flow without consent.

### 5.4 Forbidden

- Commercial use of mental health crisis data.
- Use of mental health data for employment, housing, custody, immigration adverse decisions absent specific legal basis.
- Algorithmic risk-scoring of mental health based on crisis service use.
- Surveillance through crisis services.
- Cross-border sharing exposing persons to harm.

## 6. Means restriction

### 6.1 The principle

Means restriction (limiting access to lethal means at moments of crisis) is evidence-based suicide prevention.

### 6.2 Mechanisms

- Lethal means counseling.
- Time-limited firearm transfer programs (where lawful and ethical).
- Medication packaging and access controls.
- Bridge barriers and similar infrastructure.
- Voluntary means restriction agreements.

### 6.3 Discipline

- Voluntary unless imminent danger and due process.
- Cultural and contextual sensitivity.
- Anti-confiscation absent due process.
- Rights preserved.

### 6.4 Forbidden

- Forced means restriction absent due process.
- Use of means restriction for political purposes.
- Discriminatory enforcement.

## 9. Postvention

### 9.1 The principle

Survivors of suicide loss require long-term support. Postvention is suicide prevention for those at increased risk after loss.

### 9.2 Mechanisms

- Bereavement support specifically for suicide loss.
- Peer support groups.
- Mental health support continued.
- Children's support after parental suicide.
- Anti-blame and anti-stigma.

### 9.3 Discipline

- Survivor agency.
- Long-term commitment.
- Cultural and religious sensitivity.

### 9.4 Forbidden

- Blame placed on survivors.
- Surveillance of survivors.
- Use of survivor data adversely.

## 16. The suicide prevention and MH crisis north star

How a society treats people in suicidal crisis or mental health emergency determines whether those people live and recover. CivicOS supports suicide prevention and mental health crisis response through accessible 24/7 crisis lines, mental health professional response (not police-default), trauma-informed service design, anti-stigma, sealed data, lived experience inclusion, vulnerable population support, cross-sector coordination, and postvention.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of crisis surveillance, criminalization of mental health crisis, algorithmic risk-scoring of suicidality, employment discrimination based on mental health, or stigmatization of help-seeking — it has failed at the most vulnerable moments. Capability without mental health crisis discipline is not progress; it is the institutionalization of harm to people in crisis.

When the platform supports suicide prevention and mental health crisis with discipline, dignity, accessibility, and structural protection — it earns the right to be infrastructure for societies that take care of people at their most vulnerable.

The discipline is daily. The accessibility is structural. The data is sealed. The lived experience is centered. The anti-stigma is constant. The crisis response is therapeutic, not punitive.

Lives are at stake. The platform's response matters. Anything less abandons people in crisis to compounded harm.
