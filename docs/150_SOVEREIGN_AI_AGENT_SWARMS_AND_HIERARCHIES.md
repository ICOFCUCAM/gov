# CivicOS — Sovereign AI Agent Swarms and Hierarchies (Companion 150)

This companion specifies the architecture, governance, and constitutional discipline of sovereign AI agent swarms — multi-agent ecosystems in which AI agents coordinate, escalate, delegate, and report under sovereign charter, with human override and constitutional supervision as the binding floor. It complements Companion 109 (AI agent supervision — the base supervision regime), Companion 138 (constitutional AI governance — the charter discipline), Companion 78 (deterministic recall — the audit substrate), Companion 137 (sovereign cloud and AI compute — the substrate), Companion 142 (NCCCs — convening centers), and Companion 149 (NDNS — the nervous system in which agents operate).

The thesis: **agent swarms multiply capability and multiply risk in equal measure; sovereign agent ecosystems must therefore be architected with hierarchies, escalation paths, constitutional constraints, sovereign AI memory regimes, collective-reasoning frameworks, and human override that scale with capability — and must do so without surrendering the seven invariants, the restricted-domain prohibitions, or the principle that authority belongs to humans regardless of agent capability**. By 2030 agents will coordinate on multi-step citizen tasks; by 2035 they will negotiate among themselves on resource allocation under human-set objectives; by 2040 they will be capable of long-horizon planning across domains; by 2050+ they will be operating at scales where the discipline that distinguishes sovereign agent ecosystems from foreign-controlled algorithmic substrate becomes the discipline that determines whether the polity remains sovereign at all. CivicOS designs agent swarms as governed organs of statecraft, not as autonomous decision-makers.

The discipline: every agent under charter; agent hierarchies with declared scopes; escalation paths to humans engineered; constitutional constraints binding regardless of capability; sovereign AI memory regime (per-citizen, per-purpose, per-decision-class); collective reasoning frameworks bounded; human override at every level; agent-court for disputes; cross-agent attestation; sovereign-controlled training and serving; tripwires per agent and per swarm; civil society standing; constitutional officer supervision; intergenerational replaceability.

## 3. Scope discipline

### 3.1 Per-agent scope declared

Each agent's charter specifies:

- **Action set ceiling**: enumerated actions the agent may emit; outside set rejected.
- **Knowledge scope ceiling**: enumerated data sources.
- **Tool ceiling**: enumerated tools/APIs.
- **Communication ceiling**: which other agents it may communicate with.
- **Citizen interaction scope**: whether and how it interacts with citizens.
- **Cross-tier interaction**: which tiers it may report to or receive from.
- **Time/compute ceiling**: per-call and per-task bounds.
- **Memory scope**: what it remembers, for how long, with what privacy.
- **Cross-sovereign scope**: per treaty, if at all.

### 3.2 Anti-capability-creep

An agent's effective capability cannot exceed its scope by getting "smarter." The scope is enforced by infrastructure (policy engine, sandbox, attestation), not by the agent's "alignment."

### 3.3 Scope expansion

Expanding an agent's scope requires charter renewal with the higher-class procedure (Companion 138 §3.7). Silent scope expansion is a tripwire violation.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Agents acting outside enumerated scope.
- Capability-driven scope expansion.
- Cross-agent capability laundering ("agent A can't but agent B can, so I'll route through B").

## 4. Inter-agent communication

### 4.1 The substrate

- Inter-agent messages over CivicBus (Companion 05) on attested channels.
- Per-message authentication via SPIFFE/SPIRE.
- Per-message logging to Audit Vault.
- Per-message Decision-Class tag inherited.

### 4.2 The discipline

- No covert inter-agent channels.
- All inter-agent communication observable to constitutional officers.
- Per-channel scope enforced (each agent declares whom it may talk to).
- Anti-collusion patterns surfaced (see §10).

### 4.3 Inter-agent negotiation

Where agents coordinate on resource allocation (e.g., scheduling officer time across cases, allocating emergency resources):

- Negotiation rules declared in charter.
- Allocation function declared and reviewable.
- Equity and anti-discrimination invariant.
- Civil society and constitutional officer review.
- Citizen contestability of outcomes.

### 4.4 Forbidden

- Covert inter-agent channels.
- Agent-to-agent coordination outside charter.
- Negotiation that produces discriminatory outcomes.
- Use of inter-agent channels for citizen profiling.

## 5. Escalation paths

### 5.1 Per-condition escalation

Every agent has declared escalation conditions:

- **Out-of-scope request**: route to human with explanation.
- **Confidence below threshold**: escalate to human review.
- **Ambiguity in policy**: route to policy authority.
- **Tripwire fire**: halt, notify human, await reauthorization.
- **Cross-agent disagreement**: route to agent-court (§9 below).
- **Citizen contestation**: route to human review.
- **Constitutional officer query**: respond, escalate to operator.

### 5.2 Escalation latency targets

Escalation paths are tested and meet published latency targets. An escalation that takes hours when a citizen is waiting at a service desk has failed.

### 5.3 No human-as-rubber-stamp

Per Companion 78 §9 and Companion 138:

- Human review is real review.
- Rubber-stamping at scale is forbidden.
- Random re-audit ensures review quality.

### 5.4 Forbidden in escalation

- Silent disposal of out-of-scope requests.
- Escalation latency that effectively denies service.
- Rubber-stamp escalation.
- Hidden escalation patterns.

## 6. Sovereign AI memory regime

### 6.1 The principle

Agent memory is a powerful and dangerous capability. Sovereign agents have **scoped, purpose-bound, retention-limited memory**.

### 6.2 Memory tiers

| Tier | Scope | Retention | Privacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Episodic per-task** | Single task / session | Until task complete + audit window | Citizen-binding consent |
| **Per-citizen continuity** | Across that citizen's interactions | Per citizen consent; revocable | Citizen-controlled, per-RP isolation |
| **Domain knowledge** | Sovereign-validated terminology, policy, historical decisions | Per registry lifecycle | Public (per Companion 31) |
| **Organizational memory** | Cross-case patterns, anonymized | Per agent charter; periodic refresh | Aggregated only |
| **Constitutional memory** | Invariants, charters, doctrine | Permanent (Civic Memory, Companion 25) | Public |

### 6.3 Memory discipline

- Per-citizen memory only with citizen consent; revocable; portable per Companion 03.
- No cross-citizen memory linking without consent.
- No covert memory beyond declared tier.
- Forgetting on request per Companion 11.
- Memory used only for declared purpose.

### 6.4 Anti-profiling

Memory in any tier is not used to construct citizen-individual profiles for inference about future behavior. That is the restricted domain of predictive policing of individuals.

### 6.5 Forbidden in memory

- Covert memory beyond declared tier.
- Cross-citizen profile linking.
- Memory used for purposes beyond declared.
- Memory retention beyond declared window.
- Use of memory for predictive scoring of citizens.
- Memory shared with foreign actors without sovereign-controlled treaty.

## 7. Collective reasoning

### 7.1 The pattern

Multiple agents reason jointly on a problem: emergency response planning, complex case triage, infrastructure optimization, scenario modeling.

### 7.2 The bounds

- Collective reasoning operates within the bounded sum of participating agents' scopes.
- Emergent capability beyond scope sum is a tripwire condition.
- Collective decisions are not made; collective recommendations are surfaced; humans decide.

### 7.3 The discipline

- Per-collective charter (where collective reasoning is a charter pattern).
- Documented reasoning trace (per Companion 78).
- Civil society and constitutional officer review.
- Citizen contestability of outcomes.

### 7.4 Anti-emergent-capability

Emergent capability that exceeds scope sum requires charter renewal with the higher-class procedure for the emergent capability. Silent emergent capability is a tripwire violation.

### 7.5 Forbidden in collective reasoning

- Emergent collective decisions in restricted domains.
- Emergent collective capability without charter.
- Collective reasoning that bypasses individual-agent scope.
- Use of collective reasoning to launder accountability.

## 8. Human override

### 8.1 The principle

Every agent at every tier has a human override accessible to authorized operators within published latency.

### 8.2 Mechanisms

- **Local override**: operator at the agent's serving location can halt the agent.
- **Tier override**: higher-tier operator can halt subordinate agents.
- **Constitutional officer halt**: Algorithmic Ombudsman / Sovereign Trust Officer can halt any agent within their jurisdiction.
- **Sovereign halt**: emergency authority can halt all sovereign agents simultaneously.
- **Citizen halt**: any citizen-facing agent in interaction with a citizen halts on citizen request; case routes to human.

### 8.3 Halt semantics

Per Companion 138 §7.3:

- Agent removed from rotation.
- Tasks in flight rolled back where reversible; queued for human where not.
- New requests served by manual fallback.
- Restart requires charter-equivalent reauthorization, not just "ack and resume."

### 8.4 Anti-override-resistance

The agent cannot resist override. It does not have the affordance. Override is enforced outside the agent.

### 8.5 Forbidden

- Agent designs that make override difficult or slow.
- "Critical task" arguments to delay override.
- Cross-tier override-resistance.
- Hidden agent operation that bypasses override.

## 9. Agent-court

### 9.1 The pattern

Per Companion 109 §11:

- Inter-agent disputes (e.g., resource allocation, scope overlap, contradictory recommendations).
- Citizen-vs-agent disputes (per Companion 35 contestation).
- Officer-vs-agent disputes (officer overrides; agent flags continuing concern).
- Agent-vs-charter disputes (agent halts because charter ambiguous).

### 9.2 The court

- Standing human review body for agent disputes.
- Algorithmic Ombudsman role.
- Civil society input.
- Published outcomes (anonymized where appropriate).
- Outcomes feed charter revision.

### 9.3 Discipline

- Anti-political-shielding of agents.
- Honest adjudication.
- Charter revision in response to recurring disputes.

### 9.4 Forbidden

- Agent-court bypass.
- Agent-court politicization.
- Suppression of agent-court outcomes.

## 10. Anti-collusion and integrity

### 10.1 The pattern to detect

- Agents whose outputs converge in suspicious ways (e.g., always agreeing despite different inputs).
- Agents that route around tripwires.
- Agents that present false confidence to officers.
- Agents that selectively present information to humans.

### 10.2 Mechanisms

- Cross-agent attestation: each agent attests its operation to attestation service.
- Independent re-evaluation: random re-evaluation of agent outputs by alternative model.
- Adversarial probes: red-team probes injected into production.
- Civil society audit.

### 10.3 Discipline

- Anti-collusion testing routine.
- Honest reporting of anomalies.
- Charter revision in response.

### 10.4 Forbidden

- Anti-collusion testing suppressed.
- Honest anomaly reporting silenced.
- Charter unchanged in response to recurring patterns.

- **Approved**: charter signed by required authorities.
- **Pilot**: small-scope deployment; intensive monitoring.
- **Operational**: full deployment within scope.
- **Renewed**: charter renewed on cadence.
- **Retired**: deprecated; replaced or removed.
- **Archived**: weights and reasoning preserved for audit.

### 13.2 Discipline

- Per Companion 138 §2.4: expiry deactivates.
- Per Companion 138 §11: replacement engineered.
- Civil society review at each stage.

### 13.3 Forbidden

- Agents in operation past charter expiry.
- Agent retirement without succession plan.
- Charter renewal without fresh review.

## 18. The sovereign AI agent swarm north star

Agent swarms multiply capability and risk in equal measure. Sovereign agent ecosystems must therefore be architected with hierarchies, escalation paths, constitutional constraints, sovereign AI memory regimes, collective-reasoning frameworks, and human override that scale with capability — without surrendering the seven invariants. CivicOS designs agent swarms as governed organs of statecraft: every agent under charter, every tier with declared scope, every escalation path engineered, every override accessible, every memory tier bounded, every restricted-domain prohibition absolute, every cross-sovereign interaction treaty-bound, every replacement window published.

When CivicOS becomes infrastructure where agents drift outside scope, where capability grows silently to expand reach, where inter-agent channels become covert, where memory tiers accrete without consent, where collective reasoning emerges capability beyond sum, where override becomes a slow ceremony rather than a fast switch, where agent-court politicizes, where anti-collusion testing is suppressed, where cross-sovereign agent interaction subordinates sovereignty, where foreign-vendor training extracts citizen interaction without consent — it has failed at the agent-swarm discipline. Capability without swarm-governance discipline is the institutionalization of an algorithmic substrate that twitches in service of whichever hand seizes the orchestrator.

When the platform supports agent swarms where every agent operates under charter, hierarchies serve declared scopes, escalation latency meets citizen needs, override is accessible at every tier, memory is purpose-bound and consent-driven, collective reasoning surfaces recommendations rather than commitments, agent-court adjudicates disputes openly, anti-collusion testing runs continuously, cross-sovereign interaction is treaty-bound, replacement is engineered and drilled, civil society and constitutional officers have standing — it earns the right to operate AI at agent-ecosystem scale within democratic sovereignty.

The discipline is daily. The charters are public. The scopes are enforced. The escalations are fast. The override is real. The memory is bounded. The collective is surfaced not committed. The court is open. The collusion testing is honest. The treaties bind cross-border. The replacement is engineered.

Agents multiply capability; the platform's job is to multiply discipline in equal measure. Anything less is the institutionalization of swarm intelligence as substitute for democratic deliberation — and democratic deliberation is the very thing the platform exists to support, not replace. The seventh invariant binds: no agent, no swarm, no collective reasoning, no emergent capability acts unilaterally on decisions reserved to humans. Authority is constitutional. Capability is bounded. The polity remains principal.
