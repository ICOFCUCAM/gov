# CivicOS — LGBTQ+ Communities and Constitutional Variation (Companion 87)

This companion specifies how CivicOS approaches lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, intersex, and other (LGBTQ+) communities across the diverse legal contexts in which CivicOS may be deployed. It complements Companion 22 (dignity), Companion 47 (gender equity), Companion 51 (religious affairs), Companion 48 (refugee/asylum), Companion 38 (mental health), and Companion 70 (corrections) by going deep on a domain where sovereign legal frameworks vary widely and rights protection requires deliberate platform discipline.

The thesis: **LGBTQ+ communities face deeply varied legal and social contexts across sovereigns — from full equality to criminalization — and CivicOS deployments must structurally protect rights, dignity, privacy, and safety regardless of the legal framework**. CivicOS does not impose specific policy on contested matters but commits to: protection of LGBTQ+ citizens from algorithmic outing, surveillance, persecution facilitation, and discriminatory automated decisions; sealed compartments for sensitive sexual orientation and gender identity data; refugee protection where LGBTQ+ persecution is a basis for asylum; healthcare access; anti-discrimination remedies where statutorily available; civil society protection for LGBTQ+ organizations.

The discipline: SOGI (sexual orientation, gender identity) data sealed; anti-outing structural; anti-algorithmic-discrimination strict; refugee protection per international standards; healthcare access regardless of identity; civil society organizations protected; cross-border data sharing exposing LGBTQ+ persons to persecution refused; cultural and religious accommodation within constitutional rights frameworks; vulnerable individuals protected against community pressure where applicable.

1. **Dignity universal.** LGBTQ+ citizens deserve dignity regardless of sovereign legal framework.
2. **Anti-outing structural.** Platform does not facilitate disclosure of SOGI without consent.
3. **SOGI data sealed.** Highest privacy regime.
4. **Anti-algorithmic-discrimination.** Where law prohibits, enforced strictly.
5. **Healthcare access regardless of identity.** Including trans-specific where lawful.
6. **Refugee protection per international standards.** LGBTQ+ persecution as asylum basis.
7. **Civil society protection.** LGBTQ+ organizations supported per general civil society principles.
8. **Cross-border data sharing exposing persecution refused.**
9. **Cultural and religious variation respected within rights frameworks.**
10. **Vulnerable individuals protected.** Including from community pressure.

## 2. Sovereign legal context spectrum

CivicOS deployments occur across the full spectrum:

| Context | Description | CivicOS posture |
|---|---|---|
| **Full equality** | Comprehensive anti-discrimination; same-sex marriage; trans rights | Standard service equity |
| **Partial equality** | Some protections but not all | Available protections fully implemented |
| **Decriminalized** | Same-sex relations not criminal; limited recognition | Anti-discrimination per available law |
| **No specific protection** | Neither protected nor criminalized | Universal Service Standard applies; SOGI data sealed |
| **Criminalization (limited)** | Some same-sex acts criminal; enforcement variable | Civil liberties safeguards strict; data protection paramount |
| **Criminalization (severe)** | Severe penalties; aggressive enforcement | CivicOS deployment context evaluated; rights protections critical |

CivicOS commits to structural rights protection regardless of context, while respecting sovereign authority on policy. Where deployments occur in contexts of severe persecution, the Foundation evaluates engagement carefully (per Companion 49 §13).

## 3. SOGI data discipline

### 3.1 The principle

Sexual orientation and gender identity data is among the most sensitive personal data. In many contexts, disclosure can be life-threatening.

### 3.2 Mechanisms

- SOGI data classified as Sensitive (Companion 23 §6).
- Sealed compartment storage (Companion 23 §3.2).
- Anti-cross-purpose access.
- Per-RP UID prevents cross-service correlation.
- No mandatory disclosure for service access (except where service requires, e.g., trans healthcare).
- Self-determination of disclosure.

### 3.3 Discipline

- Collection minimal.
- Use strictly limited.
- Access logged and audited.
- Anti-aggregation.

### 3.4 Forbidden

- Mandatory SOGI disclosure beyond legitimate need.
- Cross-purpose use of SOGI data.
- Algorithmic profiling based on SOGI.
- Cross-border sharing of SOGI data without consent.
- Use of SOGI data for adverse decisions in unrelated contexts.

## 4. Anti-outing

### 4.1 The principle

Platform must not facilitate disclosure of LGBTQ+ status without explicit consent.

### 4.2 Mechanisms

- Default-private SOGI status.
- Service interactions do not display SOGI status to officers absent need.
- Address and contact information protected.
- Trans citizens not outed through identity processes (legal name and gender update accessible).
- Anti-pattern-disclosure (preventing inference of SOGI through service patterns).

### 4.3 Discipline

- Anti-tracking-of-LGBTQ-services-use.
- Discretion in service delivery.
- Anti-algorithmic-disclosure.

### 4.4 Forbidden

- Officer access to SOGI status absent need.
- Outing through service interactions.
- Algorithmic disclosure of SOGI patterns.
- Use of platform for involuntary outing.

## 18. The LGBTQ+ communities north star

LGBTQ+ communities face widely varied legal and social contexts across sovereigns. CivicOS does not impose policy on contested matters but commits to structural rights protection regardless of legal framework: SOGI data sealed, anti-outing structural, anti-algorithmic-discrimination where law prohibits, healthcare access, refugee protection, civil society protection, anti-cross-border-persecution-exposure, and cultural and religious accommodation within constitutional rights.

When CivicOS becomes a tool of LGBTQ+ outing, surveillance, persecution facilitation, algorithmic discrimination, or refoulement to persecution — it has failed dignity. Capability without LGBTQ+ rights protection is not progress; it is the institutionalization of harm to vulnerable people.

When the platform protects LGBTQ+ communities through structural commitments — SOGI sealed, anti-outing, anti-discrimination where law applies, healthcare access, refugee protection, civil society standing, cross-border refoulement protection — it earns the right to be infrastructure for plural societies that respect dignity across the spectrum of identities.

The discipline is daily. The data protection is structural. The anti-outing is unconditional. The refugee protection is per international standards. The civil society protection is real. The cultural and religious variation is respected within rights frameworks.

LGBTQ+ citizens deserve dignity regardless of sovereign legal context. The platform serves their dignity through structural protections that hold even where law does not. Anything less abandons LGBTQ+ citizens to platform-mediated harm.
